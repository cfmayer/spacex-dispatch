The Morning Picture

"Fundamentally an infrastructure company"

"Yes, we are a rocket company, but fundamentally we are an infrastructure company at this point," SpaceX vice president of launch Kiko Dontchev said at last week's UP.Summit, Ars Technica reported Monday. He said Grok now finds debris in rocket parts better than human inspectors, that Falcon 9 is headed for retirement and that Starship's Florida pad is "effectively complete."

Starship Flight 15 is now on the Federal Aviation Administration's (FAA) operations advisory for Friday at 5:45 p.m. EST, with Booster 22 lifted onto Pad 2 late Monday. USSF-627 slipped a day to tonight, and SPCX gained 5.81% Monday in a broad market rally.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Booster 22 goes up on Pad 2 for the double catch

Flight 15 reaches the FAA advisory, two Vandenberg launches shift and NASA picks Starship for a 2028 demonstration.

Booster 22 was lifted for installation on Pad 2 at Starbase late Monday, NASASpaceflight posted at 10:34 p.m. EST, after waiting to cross Texas Highway 4 earlier Monday. The booster carries "33 Raptor 3 engines," the outlet noted.

Flight 15's launch and reentry were added to the FAA's operations advisory Monday morning, SpaceNotices reported. The advisory lists a launch window of 5:45 to 7:40 p.m. EST Friday, Nov. 13, and a ship reentry window of 3:56 to 7:14 p.m. EST Saturday, with backups a day later. SpaceX's mission page still says the flight is "pending regulatory approval."

Starship status

Starship last flew in late September, when Flight 14 became the first orbital Starship launch and carried Starlink V3 satellites, Ars Technica noted. Flight 15, the fourth Starship V3 flight, is to deploy 26 more V3 satellites, catch the Super Heavy booster about six and a half minutes after liftoff and, after roughly 14 orbits, try the first catch of a ship at the tower about 22.5 hours after launch, according to SpaceX. Flight 16 is expected to be the first Starship launch from Launch Complex 39A (LC-39A) in Florida, NASASpaceflight has reported (see Section IV).

USSF-627 slips to tonight

SpaceX moved the USSF-627 launch for the U.S. Space Force from Monday at 10:03 p.m. EST to Tuesday, Nov. 10, with a four-hour window opening at 9:49 p.m. EST from Space Launch Complex 4E (SLC-4E) at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California; the backup is Wednesday at 9:35 p.m. EST, SpaceX's mission page shows. No reason was given. The booster flies for the seventh time and is to land on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You. The FAA advisory lists the mission as SpaceX R-5, with a window of 9:49 p.m. to 2:32 a.m. EST.

USSF-317 moves to Friday night

USSF-317, previously set for Thursday at 11:23 p.m. EST, is now targeted for Friday, Nov. 13, at 11:09 p.m. EST, with a backup on Saturday at 10:55 p.m. EST, according to SpaceX; no reason was given. Its booster makes its 11th flight after NROL-105, USSF-385 and eight Starlink missions. The FAA advisory still lists Thursday night as the primary window and Friday night as the backup.

NASA picks Starship for a cislunar communications demo

NASA selected SpaceX for its Cislunar Communications Deployment Demonstration: under a task order on an existing lunar logistics contract, SpaceX will launch communications satellites aboard a Starship to the region between Earth and the Moon in 2028, the agency announced Saturday at Kennedy Space Center. SpaceX was also one of eight companies named to CLPS 2, the next phase of NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services, a 10-year contract with a combined maximum value of $10 billion, and some new lunar transport demonstrations will fly on Falcon 9.

III · Starlink

Brussels Airlines flies its first Starlink jet

Lufthansa Group's rollout reaches Belgium; SpaceX seeks Australian spectrum; stock LTE modules link from orbit.

Flight SN2257 from Brussels to Copenhagen on Monday was Brussels Airlines' first flight with Starlink Wi-Fi, flown by the Airbus A320neo OO-SBA, the airline said in a release it sent from 12,000 meters up. The service is free in every booking class for passengers with a Travel ID or Miles & More account, with 15 aircraft to be equipped by the first half of 2027. Starlink is equipping about 850 aircraft across the Lufthansa Group's airlines. "Starlink is now delivering fast, reliable internet onboard," @Starlink posted at 10:46 a.m. EST Monday.

SpaceX presses Australia for 2 GHz spectrum

SpaceX is pushing the Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA) to open the 2 GHz mobile-satellite band so phones can connect straight to Starlink satellites, ChannelNews reported. "Satellite connectivity is not a replacement for terrestrial networks; it complements them by providing another way for Australians to stay connected when they need it most," said David Goldman, SpaceX's vice president of satellite policy, who visited Australia this month. The ACMA said it is still weighing licensing and allocation and will finish the process before a new Universal Outdoor Mobile Obligation takes effect in December 2027.

Quick Hit

Japan. Starlink will bring high-speed internet to vacation homes run by SANU in remote and rural parts of Japan, @Starlink posted in Japanese at 12:45 a.m. EST Tuesday (translated), quoting SANU's plan to use it in a new building called SHELL.

Standard LTE modules reach Direct to Cell

Two off-the-shelf LTE Cat.1 bis modules from Fibocom, the LE270-EU and LE271-GL, registered on SpaceX's Direct to Cell network in New Zealand on standard firmware with no satellite-specific hardware, the company said Sunday. Outdoors they reached about 2.2 and 2.0 Mbps down and 0.3 Mbps up, with registration taking 1 to 30 seconds; indoors downlink fell to about 0.5 Mbps.

IV · Florida, Texas & the Road Ahead

Falcon 9 is "firmly on the road to retirement"

SpaceX's launch chief says Starship's Florida pad is ready for its first barge deliveries in December.

"Falcon 9, I love that rocket to heart," Dontchev said at the UP.Summit in Florida, according to Ars Technica. "But it's still not fully reusable. We still throw that second stage away every single flight. Starship—we're going to catch both the first stage and the second stage." A partially reusable rocket works for 100 launches a year, he said, but not for a company that wants to launch thousands a year.

He said a single Starship launch from Florida will carry up to 20 times the bandwidth of a single Falcon 9; Florida's trajectories avoid propellant-costly dogleg maneuvers, Ars noted. "Pad 39A is effectively complete," he said. "We're going to transport boosters and ships over barge here to the Cape. We're hoping to actually do the first transports this year in December, and then maybe even fly by the end of the year." SpaceX is developing two more pads in Florida.

Dontchev said Mars remains central: "Without AI, I think we still would have gotten to the Moon. I think we still get to Mars with SI. We're going to get there so much faster."

Boca Chica park sale paused

Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa said Thursday that she had asked state officials to hold off on any sale of Boca Chica State Park to SpaceX until she takes office in January, and the Texas Parks and Wildlife Commission dropped the item from its agenda, the San Antonio Current reported. The department had been considering selling about 1,300 acres of the park next to Starbase so SpaceX could expand. "We can have a strong working relationship with Elon Musk and his companies, but it has to be on our terms," Hinojosa said on CNN.

V · AI & Compute

Grok beats human inspectors at spotting debris

SpaceX says AI now reviews borescope video for foreign objects; Musk says Grok 4.8's stack was rewritten in C and C++.

SpaceX has largely automated the search for foreign object debris (FOD) in aerospace parts with Grok, Dontchev said. "We'll have an inspector go in and borescope a tube, and we find that humans are about 94 percent perfect (at) finding a piece of it. Grok analyzing the same video is 99.9 percent perfect," he said, as reported by Ars Technica. It also checks Starlink terminals, he said. Musk quoted a post sharing a clip of the remarks at 10:04 p.m. EST Monday with one word: "Grok."

Grok 4.8 rebuilt from scratch

"We have dumped JAX/SGLang and rewritten the entire RL and inference stack from scratch in C/C++, which took some extra time," Musk wrote at 4:35 a.m. EST Tuesday, answering a user who asked when to expect Grok 4.8. "Training (aka pre-training) is done. ... Now, 4.8 is in RL and the evals are starting to climb. We will do a soft rollout to Bot users later this week and see how it goes." He added that the model "is not yet incredible, but, this time, the foundations are built right."

"At SpaceX, we write our own headers and calculate our own time (includes leap seconds)," Musk added at 5:14 a.m. EST. Twenty minutes later he posted: "Turns out that the Starlink software, built for managing the movement of vast amounts of data through space and to the ground, is also very well suited to training super intelligence."
