The Morning Picture

A share for every Trump Account, and an FCC license for a million data-center satellites

SpaceX said on Wednesday it will contribute one newly issued Class A share to every active Trump Account, the federal investment accounts for American children, and Elon Musk said he will match it from his own holdings: the company's share is to be transferred next month, his when his lock-up expires in June. The same day the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) granted SpaceX's Starmind application, "the first authorization in U.S. history for a constellation of data centers in space," for up to 1 million satellites.

SpaceX also asked Cochise County, Arizona, to keep a Dec. 9 hearing on its $50 billion-plus supercomputing campus. SPCX fell as much as 10% early amid merger worries, Yahoo Finance reported, then closed up 1.3% at $127.44. US markets are closed today for Thanksgiving; a Falcon 9 is set to launch a Cygnus XL cargo ship to the space station on Sunday.

I · Shares for the Next Generation

"I recommend holding on to your shares"

One share from SpaceX and one from Musk for every active Trump Account; Gwynne Shotwell has pledged more than 2 million of her own.

SpaceX announced a commitment to contribute one newly issued share of its Class A common stock to every currently active Trump Account, the tax-advantaged accounts that help American children build long-term savings, and said Musk will personally match the number of shares the company donates. The release said President and Chief Operating Officer Gwynne Shotwell has separately pledged a personal gift of over 2 million SpaceX shares. "We believe ownership of the company creating these systems should extend to the next generation of Americans," SpaceX posted at 2:21 p.m. EST Wednesday.

"Every child in America will receive two SpaceX shares, one from the company and one from me personally," Musk wrote at 10:43 p.m. EST, and at 1:01 a.m. EST Thursday he added: "The share from SpaceX will be transferred next month. The share directly from me will be transferred when my lockup expires in June next year." "I recommend holding on to your shares," he wrote at 3:20 a.m. EST.

How many accounts

The White House said in October that nearly 70 million Trump Accounts had been created, more than 60 million through auto-enrollment, but it is unclear how many count as active, CNBC reported. Under Treasury guidelines, families cannot refuse stock gifted to the accounts and generally cannot sell it until the earlier of five years or the end of the year the child turns 17. At Wednesday's close the pledge is worth about $18 billion, The Indian Express reported. Replying to a post that said foster youth in 24 states would not have access to the shares, Musk wrote at 3:34 a.m. EST: "We will try to get the foster kids shares anyway."

Starship status

Booster 25 and Ship 46 kept taking shape at Starbase on Wednesday: a forward section of Booster 25 was stacked in Mega Bay 1 and Ship 46's common-dome section was stacked in Mega Bay 2, the Starship Desk log shows, with both moves marked confirmed. Flight 16 remains listed no earlier than Dec. 10 from Starbase Pad 2, a target SpaceX has not confirmed.

III · Starmind, Compute & Starlink

The FCC clears a million orbiting data centers

The first US license for data centers in space; the first satellite is due by 2033, the full constellation by 2040.

The FCC said on Wednesday it had granted "the first authorization in U.S. history for a constellation of data centers in space," allowing SpaceX to deploy 1,000,000 orbital compute modules. "By moving compute capacity beyond Earth, SpaceX and the many competitors working on their own orbital data centers are meeting the moment," Chairman Brendan Carr said. Replying to Carr at 11:41 a.m. EST, Musk wrote: "Much appreciated. This will be a great asset to America and the world."

The Space Bureau's order puts the satellites between 500 and 1,002 kilometers up and allows radio links in the 18.8–19.3 GHz and 28.6–29.1 GHz bands only as a backup to the satellites' laser links, on a non-interference basis. SpaceX must have one satellite operating by Nov. 25, 2033, 10% of the constellation by 2035, 50% by 2038 and the rest by Nov. 25, 2040. Musk said at a Baron Capital event that he expects the first Starmind spacecraft, built with Nvidia, to launch in the third quarter of 2027, Spaceflight Now reported: "We'll start off slow, but then it will ramp to significant volume probably in '28 and then very large volume in '29."

Colossus 3: "no gas plant"

SpaceX said at 12:07 p.m. EST that its supercomputing site in Cochise County, Arizona, has "no gas plant, no industrial water use, and a dry-cooling closed-loop water system," with an investment of $50 billion-plus, 1,000 permanent jobs, up to 5,500 construction jobs and $3.6 billion to upgrade the grid; residential rates for Sulphur Springs Valley Electric Cooperative (SSVEC) members "may decrease by up to 40%." It asked the county commission to keep its planned Dec. 9 meeting "and let residents be heard." The project page lists nine computing buildings drawing up to six gigawatts and a 24-gigawatt-hour battery system. No retail power can flow until the Arizona Corporation Commission approves the supply agreement, and no energy services agreement has been completed, AZ Big Media reported.

Quick Hit

Kazakhstan. Less than two months after Starlink Mobile launched in Kazakhstan, more than 3 million Beeline customers are using it in cellular dead zones, Starlink posted at 1:59 p.m. EST.
