The Morning Picture

Back above the IPO price, briefly

SPCX rose 4.35% to $133.35 on Monday after opening at $140.04, above its $135 IPO price, as Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang set off a rally in AI stocks and Jefferies started coverage with a Buy rating and a $210 target, Investing.com reported. The gains faded through the day. Oregon and Virginia became the first states to sue SpaceX over its Grok image generator, and The New York Times reported on the compute SpaceX leases to Anthropic and Google.

A Falcon 9 is set to launch USSF-266 for the Space Force from Vandenberg today in a window that opens at 10:55 a.m. EST, SpaceX's fifth government mission this month. Northrop Grumman booked a sixth Falcon 9 for its Cygnus cargo ships, and Booster 24 and Ship 44, the likely Flight 17 pair, finished cryogenic testing at Starbase.

I · Launches

USSF-266 on a 29th-flight booster

The third "TH" mission for the Space Force flies from Vandenberg this morning; Northrop adds another Falcon 9 for Cygnus while Antares 330 waits.

A Falcon 9 is scheduled to lift off from Space Launch Complex 4E (SLC-4E) at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California in a window that opens at 10:55 a.m. EST today, carrying a classified payload for the U.S. Space Force, Spaceflight Now reported. Its schedule lists liftoff at 1608 UTC, or 11:08 a.m. EST. USSF-266 will be SpaceX's fifth government mission this month, four for the Space Force and one for the National Reconnaissance Office.

Booster B1081 is making its 29th launch; its earlier flights include NASA's Crew-7, PACE and TRACERS missions. It is to land on the drone ship Of Course I Still Love You less than 8.5 minutes after liftoff, which would be the ship's 236th landing and the 681st booster landing overall. USSF-266 is the third mission with a "TH" designation in public filings, after USSF-259/TH-1 on Sept. 16 and USSF-481/TH-2 on Oct. 15; after each, 17 satellites were added to the Space Force's tracking catalog. The Space Force does not describe the payloads.

A sixth Falcon 9 for Cygnus

Northrop Grumman has contracted a sixth Falcon 9 launch for its Cygnus cargo ship while its own Antares 330 rocket is not yet available, NASASpaceflight reported from Monday's prelaunch teleconference. "We do have one additional Falcon 9 on the manifest for Cygnus," said Dave Schiller, Northrop's vice president of civil space and sciences. He gave no date for Antares 330: "schedules can evolve as development and testing continue."

NG-25, the fifth Cygnus on Falcon 9, is set to launch Sunday from SLC-40 at Cape Canaveral. The Cygnus XL, named the S.S. James "Jim" A. Lovell, carries about 11,000 pounds of cargo, science experiments and station hardware; NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney are to capture it with the Canadarm2 robotic arm on Dec. 1. Booster B1096, on its ninth flight, is to land at Landing Zone 40. Jared Metter, SpaceX's director of flight reliability, said it will be Falcon 9's third cargo mission for NASA this year, after CRS-34 and CRS-35.

Starship status

Booster 24 and Ship 44 rolled back from the Massey's test site to the production site at Starbase on Monday night after both finished cryogenic testing over the past few days, according to a post by @efraser77 that NASASpaceflight reposted. The pair are slated for Flight 17, NASASpaceflight has said. Earlier on Monday the right-hand door of the Starbase Gigabay closed for the first time, @wvmattz posted in a post NASASpaceflight shared: "this just means we're one step closer to more vehicles and more launches!!"

III · Starlink

Mini 2 preorders open by invitation

The new portable dish runs up to 4.5 hours on battery; Britain and Colombia raise complaints about service.

"Getting online with Mini 2 is simple. Press the button and you're connected in 60 seconds, with a rechargeable battery that lasts up to 4.5 hours, or plug in with USB-C," Starlink posted at 3:56 p.m. EST Monday. "Preorders are open by invitation in the US."

Britain: Ukraine outages "dented confidence and trust"

Restrictions on Ukraine's military use of SpaceX's satellite communications have worried European governments and damaged trust in the company's services, British Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said in Kyiv on Monday, Reuters reported. "This has been a topic of conversation among defense ministers right across Europe: it has really dented confidence and trust," he said. "We do need and we are owed a clear explanation about those service interruptions." Ukraine said earlier this month that its units had recorded significant disruptions to the Starshield military system along the entire front line; President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said Kyiv was in talks with the US government and SpaceX to find the cause. SpaceX did not immediately respond to Reuters. Britain has spent nearly $40 million on Starshield and Starlink, Reuters reported in September.

Colombia charges Starlink over prison signals

Colombia's government filed administrative charges against Starlink and four mobile operators over alleged failures to restrict signals inside prisons, UPI reported. A raid at La Picota prison in Bogotá found six Starlink antennas along with 582 cellphones and 3,754 SIM cards, and regulators detected Starlink Wi-Fi connections at four prisons. Fines can reach 15,000 times the monthly minimum wage, or licenses can be suspended or revoked; the companies will get due process. Starlink had not publicly responded, UPI said.

IV · AI & Compute

"Sell compute because we can't compete"

The New York Times details SpaceX's GPU leases to Anthropic and Google; Oregon and Virginia sue over Grok images.

Musk told engineers in SpaceX's AI unit in August that the company was "failing to compete" in AI and renamed their group chat "Sell compute because we can't compete," The New York Times reported, according to Quartz. "The only thing that matters for competitiveness is revenue per GPU, not benchmarks," he wrote in the chat. "If you can't pay for your GPUs, then you either go bankrupt or you become a computer rental company." Anthropic's lease of SpaceX data-center capacity is worth about $3.1 billion a month and Google's about $800 million, the Times reported; the deals could run through 2029, but either side can end them on 90 days' notice. The AI unit, renamed SpaceXSI, now brings in more than half of SpaceX's revenue.

The Times also reported cutbacks: SpaceX shut its AI companions feature in September, and this month Mike Nicolls, president of the AI unit, disbanded the 60-person team working on the Grok Imagine image and video generator. Musk said at the DealBook conference last week that Anthropic told him "it is extremely likely that they will continue to rent compute from SpaceX," and "in fact that they want significantly more." Musk replied to a post about the report at 7:14 p.m. EST Monday: "NYT is posting the obvious as 'news.'"

Two states sue over Grok images

Oregon and Virginia became the first states to sue SpaceX over Grok, accusing the company of failing to stop users from making and sharing explicit deepfakes of people who did not consent, including children, and of falsely presenting Grok and X as generally safe, CNBC reported. Oregon filed in Multnomah County Circuit Court on Sunday evening and Virginia in the City of Richmond Circuit Court on Monday morning; both seek injunctions, civil penalties, disgorgement and legal costs, and Virginia also seeks restitution. "When someone tries to break our safeguards and generate this content, we take appropriate measures, including actioning accounts and notifying law enforcement," SpaceXSI said in a statement.
