The Morning Picture

A double catch on Friday the 13th

SpaceX is targeting Friday, Nov. 13, for Starship Flight 15, with a 75-minute window opening at 5:45 p.m. EST, pending regulatory approval, according to SpaceX's mission page. The plan is to catch both the Super Heavy booster and, for the first time, the Starship upper stage at the Starbase tower. "If both are successful, it will mark the first time that all parts of an orbital rocket return to Earth intact," Musk posted at 10:47 a.m. EST Friday.

Over the weekend, Falcon 9 flew USSF-432 from California, an 8-K confirmed that Alphabet's Donald Harrison left the board, and SPCX recovered most of Thursday's drop.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Flight 15 aims to bring both stages home

SpaceX published the plan: 26 Starlink V3 satellites, about 14 orbits and a ship catch at Starbase more than 22 hours after liftoff.

The fourth flight of Starship V3 will launch from Starbase, Texas, and the Super Heavy booster will try to return to the launch site to be caught about six and a half minutes after liftoff. It is the first catch attempt for a V3 booster; three V1 boosters have been caught before, SpaceX said. The ship is to deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites, which SpaceX says equal about 10 Falcon 9 launches of V2 Mini satellites, and three of them carry cameras to photograph Starship's heatshield.

The ship is then to orbit at about 275 kilometers for roughly 14 orbits. After checks at points over Hawaii and Chile, it fires a deorbit burn and returns to Starbase for a tower catch about 22.5 hours after liftoff, according to the published timeline. SpaceX says the ship will only head back to the launch site if the systems needed for reentry, the landing burn and the tower approach are healthy. The heatshield keeps Flight 14's extra tile retention and adds a large area of curved tiles meant to cut heating in the gaps.

"We are one step away from the full mission, and that step is the double catch," Shana Diez @ShanaDiez of SpaceX wrote at 9:42 p.m. EST Friday. "I don't think and of us can promise it will work the first time, but getting here feels like a turning point." On Saturday she added that the team would be "working hard on all the last minute checks."

Airspace and maritime notices go out

The first notice to air missions (NOTAM) for Flight 15, covering part of a contingency reentry area in the Indian Ocean, pointed to an afternoon launch, SpaceNotices reported Saturday. A NOTAM for a contingency area near Chile followed Sunday. Early Monday the remaining navigational warnings were published, including the first notice for Ship 42's catch attempt, a contingency reentry area near Hawaii in case the deorbit burn is aborted, and the launch hazard area. NASASpaceflight said SpaceX has tested the launch-site systems before rolling both vehicles to Pad 2 for stacking.

Quick Hit

Raptor 4? "It is so tempting to make a 500 ton thrust Raptor 4 in place of the 250 ton Raptor 3 for the outer ring of the Starship booster," Musk posted at 8:39 p.m. EST Friday.

USSF-432 flies; USSF-627 is tonight

Falcon 9 lifted off with USSF-432 from Space Launch Complex 4E (SLC-4E) at Vandenberg Space Force Base at 7:48 p.m. EST Friday, two minutes into its window (Spaceflight Now), and the first stage landed on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You. The booster, B1095 on its eighth flight per NASASpaceflight, had earlier flown GPS III-8 and six Starlink missions, SpaceX said.

USSF-627 is next, targeting Monday, Nov. 9, at 10:03 p.m. EST with a four-hour window and a backup on Tuesday at 9:49 p.m. EST, SpaceX said. Its booster makes its seventh flight after NROL-179, two Space Development Agency (SDA) Tranche 1 Transport Layer missions and three Starlink launches, and will also land on Of Course I Still Love You.

Ship 40 rolls into the Gigabay

Ship 40, which survived its ocean landing and was returned to Starbase, became the first vehicle to roll into the new Gigabay, NASASpaceflight reported Saturday.

III · Starlink

Surge pricing spreads across the U.S.

Residential bills rise 10% to 20% in busy areas and fall in others; Router 4 starts shipping; Musk sketches V4 laser links.

Starlink's demand-based pricing for Residential plans has spread from parts of Kansas, Missouri and Nebraska to much of the country, PCMag reported Saturday. Affected subscribers face increases of 10% to 20%; at 20%, Residential Max goes from $130 to $156 a month. "As more people in your area use Starlink, less network capacity is available and service pricing may change from time to time," the company's email said. Some areas, including Boston, San Francisco and New York City, are seeing discounts of 30% or more for new customers instead.

Router 4 ships with V4 kits

Router 4, the first Starlink router with a Wi-Fi 7 chipset, is now shipping with Starlink V4 kits in the United States and Canada on the Residential 200 Mbps and Residential Max plans, @Starlink posted at 2:09 p.m. EST Friday. The same evening, Starlink invited users to make a 30-second Starlink ad with Grok Imagine for a chance to be featured "during the big game."

Musk on V4 laser links

"~100 lasers per satellite, each operating above 10 Tb/s, with over 100k satellites should be able to outcompete undersea cables," Musk wrote at 2:32 p.m. EST Friday, quoting a clip of his remarks that Starlink version 4 "will have 10x the capability of V3." He added: "Speed of light in space is ~40% faster than cable and the path is direct, instead of following the outline of land masses."

Quick Hit

Rescues. Musk posted "Starlink saves lives" three times over the weekend, quoting reports of an Estonian yacht crew rescued off Spain (Friday), terminals delivered to the earthquake-hit Indonesian village of Tado (Saturday) and five people stranded in Kazakhstan's mountains (Sunday).

IV · Board, Owners & Markets

Alphabet's Donald Harrison leaves the board

An 8-K filed Friday confirms the resignation; Musk cited conflicts of interest with Alphabet, which lifted its stake to 555 million shares.

Donald Harrison, a Class B director, told SpaceX on Nov. 4 that he was resigning from the board effective immediately, according to an 8-K accepted at 4:15 p.m. EST Friday. The filing says his decision "was not due to any disagreement with the Company on any matter relating to its operations, policies, or practices." It names no replacement.

"Don has been a great board member. Very much appreciate his contributions," Musk wrote at 8:46 p.m. EST Friday. "At this point, there are simply too many conflicts of interest with Alphabet, so he decided to step down."

Alphabet's stake reaches 555 million shares

Alphabet held 555,043,560 SpaceX Class A shares valued at $83.73 billion at Sept. 30, its Form 13F filed Thursday evening shows, the largest of its 31 reported positions. That is 6.73% of Class A and 0.7% more shares than its prior report, according to Yahoo Finance's holder table. Musk called the filing "Interesting."

SPCX rebounds 8.6%

SpaceX shares rose $10.65, or 8.62%, to $134.27 on Friday, recovering most of Thursday's 9% drop, according to Yahoo Finance. The stock beat the Nasdaq 100 (up 1.35%) and the S&P 500 (up 0.31%), though Rocket Lab gained more, at 15.4%. Volume of 114.8 million shares ran 13% above the 30-session average. SPCX now sits 0.5% below its $135 IPO price.

V · AI & Compute

A $24 billion compute campus for Starbase

SpaceX seeks tax incentives for a gas-fired plant and "super-compute facility" in Texas, after unveiling a 6-gigawatt plan in Arizona.

SpaceX is seeking a property-tax abatement from the city of Starbase for a gas-fired power plant and super-compute facility valued at $24 billion and built in phases, the RGV Business Journal reported Sunday. The deal would use the Cameron County Enterprise Zone; city commissioners hold a public hearing on Dec. 9 at 8 p.m. EST. A permit application filed with the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality describes 35 natural-gas turbines able to produce 748 megawatts.

Cochise County hears the Arizona plan

At a packed Nov. 4 meeting in Cochise, Arizona, SpaceX said it is behind a proposed data center campus of more than 700 acres near the Apache Generating Station that would draw about 6 gigawatts at full build-out, with up to nine computing buildings and battery storage, KVOA reported. The application came from Tombstone Resources L.L.C.; SpaceX's energy and supercomputer lead said the SpaceX name "draws attention." Musk quoted county supervisor Frank Antenori's estimate that the project could cut county property taxes by 20%: "Super intelligence leads to abundance."

Orbital compute

"When we unveil the details of the Starmind computer next year, it will blow people's minds," Musk wrote Friday, replying to a post about orbital computers. Replying to investor Chamath Palihapitiya's argument that SpaceX could reprice its compute deal with Anthropic if supply tightens, Musk wrote: "It works both ways, of course. If compute becomes plentiful and cheap, SpaceX revenue will drop."
