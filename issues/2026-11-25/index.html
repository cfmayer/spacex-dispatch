The Morning Picture

SpaceX turns down $738 million in broadband grants as shares slide 5.7%

SpaceX will serve the rural locations Starlink won under the federal Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment (BEAD) program without taking the roughly $738 million it was awarded, under a model agreement the Commerce Department's broadband agency published on Monday; new subscribers at those locations get their equipment free. "Starlink," Elon Musk wrote in reply. SPCX fell 5.65% to $125.81 on Tuesday as AI stocks gave back Monday's Nvidia rally, and Yahoo Finance noted the company "had no news of its own to explain the drop."

A Falcon 9 launched the USSF-266 mission for the Space Force from Vandenberg at 11:08 a.m. EST Tuesday on a booster making its 29th flight. The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) will vote on Dec. 17 on a proposal to restrict foreign satellite operators whose home countries restrict American ones, a move aimed largely at European spectrum plans that have shut out SpaceX.

I · Launches

USSF-266 flies on a 29th-flight booster

The Space Force mission lifted off on time from Vandenberg; next come USSF-380 on Saturday night and the NG-25 Cygnus on Sunday.

A Falcon 9 launched the USSF-266 mission from Space Launch Complex 4E (SLC-4E) at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California at 11:08 a.m. EST Tuesday, Spaceflight Now posted, matching the liftoff time on its schedule. The payload is classified. "Liftoff!" SpaceX posted at 11:09 a.m. EST, and ten minutes later: "Falcon 9's first stage lands on the Of Course I Still Love You droneship" (post). The booster, B1081, was on its 29th flight, NASASpaceflight confirmed; its earlier missions include NASA's Crew-7, PACE and TRACERS, Spaceflight Now reported before the launch. USSF-266 was SpaceX's fifth government mission this month and the third with a "TH" designation in public filings; the Space Force does not describe the payloads. SpaceX's mission page has the webcast.

Two launches this weekend

The next Falcon 9 from Vandenberg, USSF-380 for the Space Force, is listed for 11:39 p.m. EST Saturday, and NG-25, Northrop Grumman's Cygnus XL cargo ship for the International Space Station, for 10:47 a.m. EST Sunday from SLC-40 at Cape Canaveral, according to Launch Library 2. NASA astronauts are to capture the Cygnus with the station's robotic arm on Dec. 1, NASA said.

Starship status

Starbase's Pad 2 is being readied for a Booster 23 static fire in early December rather than this week, local tracker TrackingTheSB reported, according to the Starship Desk log, which lists Flight 16's target as no earlier than Dec. 10 from an October navigation advisory; SpaceX has not announced a date or pad. Two pending FCC temporary-authority applications cited there list the next two Starship missions, Starlink G31-3 and G31-4, as launching from Starbase, Texas. A Raptor 3 engine, serial 205, was seen heading out for testing at McGregor, Texas, @SpaceRhin0 posted in a post NASASpaceflight shared at 6:47 p.m. EST Tuesday.

III · Starlink

No subsidy, same obligations

SpaceX keeps its BEAD locations at a $0 award, must still meet the program's speed and testing rules, and hands out equipment free.

The National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA) on Monday published a model agreement for low-Earth-orbit satellite providers that agree to meet BEAD service and performance-testing requirements at the locations they won but decline the subsidies. The provider must deliver BEAD-required speeds, supply customer equipment to BEAD subscribers at no extra cost, offer a low-cost service option and report every six months. "SpaceX has agreed to abide by BEAD service and performance testing requirements at all locations awarded under a state or territory's Final Proposal at no cost to the BEAD program," NTIA Administrator Arielle Roth said, as quoted by Broadband Breakfast via the Benton Institute, which put the forgone funding at $738 million. A senior agency official said SpaceX approached NTIA with the idea after struggling with differing state requirements. The monthly subscription is not waived, Teslarati reported; it said administration officials put the amount at about $636 million. Musk quoted a summary of the deal at 6:00 p.m. EST Tuesday with one word: "Starlink."

Locations can be reassigned, not penalized

SpaceX must deliver at least 100 Mbps for downloads and 20 Mbps for uploads, and the agreement exempts it from BEAD rules on procurement, reporting and financial auditing, Quartz reported, citing Teslarati. States cannot impose sanctions on SpaceX for missing the targets; they can only hand the locations back to the program to be awarded again. Several states that had already contracted with SpaceX have had those deals scrapped. Arizona, for instance, had awarded SpaceX $17.1 million to serve 10,749 locations. Starlink was first allocated nearly $661 million in BEAD funds after the administration reopened much of the bidding to wireless and satellite providers, and in January SpaceX said the program's conditions might make its involvement "untenable," PCMag reported, according to Quartz. NTIA said states that decline the model agreement must find another provider at a price similar to what their Final Proposals approved.

30 African countries, 66,866 ships

"Starlink is now approved in 30 of 54 countries in Africa!" Musk posted at 7:19 p.m. EST Tuesday, quoting a summary that counted 15 more countries on the upcoming launch list. At 4:06 a.m. EST Wednesday he quoted a post by Steph Debour saying Starlink is now on 66,866 ships, against 1,662 for OneWeb, its closest low-orbit rival.

IV · AI, Compute & the Merger

Trading like a neocloud

An AI-stock pullback and merger positioning weighed on SPCX; Mizuho named it a top pick.

SpaceX fell as companies that rent out AI computing power gave back part of Monday's Nvidia rally, Yahoo Finance reported: CoreWeave dropped 13% and Nebius 11% at midday after Hewlett Packard Enterprise chief executive Antonio Neri said on Monday evening's earnings call that its financing arm "has not provided financing to neocloud or high-risk customers." SpaceX's Super Intelligence segment, which rents data-center compute to AI companies, brought in $7.56 billion of its $14.6 billion of third-quarter revenue. It spent $19.6 billion on capital expenditures in the quarter, nearly triple a year earlier, and expects $35 billion to $40 billion in the fourth quarter. Starlink's Connectivity segment earned $2.37 billion of SpaceX's $2.79 billion of third-quarter operating income, which the report said softened the fall. Analysts expect revenue to climb from $49.1 billion in 2026 to $336 billion in 2028 and free cash flow of negative $140.7 billion in 2027, according to the report, which also noted that one customer supplied 34.5% of third-quarter revenue and that the stock traded at about 44 times forward earnings at Monday's close.

"Classic pre-deal positioning"

In The Motley Fool, Lee Samaha tied the decline to a Financial Times report that five directors, two from SpaceX and three from Tesla, had been appointed to committees discussing a possible merger. In an all-stock deal, he wrote, investors tend to buy the company expected to be acquired (Tesla) and sell the acquirer (SpaceX); Tesla closed down 0.2%.

Mizuho: "ultimate vertical integration play"

Mizuho kept an Outperform rating and a $205 target on SpaceX after a transfer of coverage and named it, with Firefly, its favorite for space and AI exposure in aerospace and defense, TheFly reported late Monday. The analyst called SpaceX the "ultimate vertical integration play."

V · Filings: SEC, FAA & FCC

The FCC takes aim at Europe's satellite rules

A Dec. 17 vote on satellite "reciprocity"; no new SEC filings or FAA notices since Tuesday morning.

FCC

The FCC will consider a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on "Ensuring Satellite Market Access Reciprocity" at its Dec. 17 open meeting (10:30 a.m. EST; GN Docket No. 26-48). "The proposal seeks comment on applying the same types of restrictions to foreign satellite providers operating here that their home nations apply to American satellite providers operating there," Chairman Brendan Carr wrote in a blog post. The FCC singled out the European Union's Digital Networks Act and a plan for EU-wide 2 GHz mobile-satellite spectrum, The Register reported. "This has locked out companies like SpaceX and Amazon Leo from attaining access to spectrum that is highly valuable for satellite direct-to-device services," CCS Insight analyst Joe Gardiner told the site. British operators could also face limits: the FCC has complained that the UK Space Agency spends most of its budget, about 75% in 2023/24, on European Space Agency contracts that American companies cannot bid for. The same meeting will take up a "light licensing" order for satellite spectrum sharing, Broadband Breakfast reported.
