The Morning Picture

SpaceX catches a Starship from orbit; the booster is lost

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) cleared Starship Flight 15 on Friday afternoon, and the rocket lifted off from Starbase at 5:47 p.m. EST. Super Heavy Booster 22 ran low on fuel during its boostback burn, aborted its tower catch and was destroyed over the Gulf, but Ship 42 deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, circled Earth 14 times and, about 22.5 hours after launch, was caught by the tower's arms at about 4:20 p.m. EST Saturday, Spaceflight Now reported. It was the first catch of a Starship upper stage.

On Sunday, a United Launch Alliance Vulcan carrying 40 Amazon Leo satellites failed 10 minutes into flight. USSF-317 slipped another day to Wednesday night, and Citi raised its SPCX price target to $280 on Monday morning. The stock closed Friday, before the launch, down 0.59% at $139.11.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Ship 42 comes home to the chopsticks

Flight 15 reached orbit, deployed its Starlink payload and returned to Starbase for the first ship catch; Booster 22 diverted to the Gulf after an early boostback cutoff.

FAA approval arrived at 2:00 p.m. EST Friday, NASASpaceflight posted; the agency issued a revised license, VOL 23-129 Rev. 10.0, authorizing a single Flight 15 launch and the associated Starship reentry, Seeking Alpha reported. Booster 22 and Ship 42 lifted off from Starbase Pad 2 at 5:47 p.m. EST, two minutes into the 75-minute window, Spaceflight Now said.

Booster 22 diverts

"Super Heavy has diverted to an offshore splashdown in the Gulf," SpaceX posted at 5:55 p.m. The booster relit 32 of its planned 33 Raptor engines for the boostback burn, which "ended early due to low fuel mass, with the root cause under investigation," SpaceX's flight summary says. Its guidance system triggered an automated abort, steered it to an offshore landing point, and the flight termination system fired when the landing burn shut down. "It was not a GNC issue that caused us not to come back. GNC triggered that abort; it was doing what it was supposed to do," SpaceX's Dan Huot said Saturday, correcting his webcast remark, Space.com reported.

Elon Musk blamed "slight underperformance of engines on this flight": "The booster is meant to land with tiny residuals, so the solution is to improve engine performance and performance consistency (underway with Raptor V3.2), as well as adjust flight software to do MECO1 about one second earlier," he wrote at 11:56 a.m. EST Saturday, adding that "this booster loss does not change our timeline to reach high volume, fully reusable Starship flights." Diverting protected the pad: "We have the next booster almost ready for flight, but losing the tower or launch mount would potentially cost us 2 to 3 months," he said.

Fourteen orbits, then the catch

Ship 42 fired a single Raptor to enter an orbit of about 275 kilometers (SpaceX), and at 7:06 p.m. EST SpaceX posted: "Payload deploy complete. All 26 @Starlink V3 satellites are in orbit." Early Saturday SpaceX passed up the off-ramp to a splashdown off Chile, Spaceflight Now reported. "Teams have polled go to return Starship to the launch site," SpaceX posted at 2:18 p.m.; the deorbit burn followed at about 3:25 p.m.

The ship reentered over the Pacific and crossed Mexico, Spaceflight Now reported, then flew a final swoop over the Gulf and lit three Raptors for a landing flip into the tower's arms, the flight summary says. "Mechazilla catches Starship!" SpaceX posted at 4:21 p.m. "That was the holy grail of spaceflight, plummeting through fire to be snatched out of the air by steel," Huot said on the webcast. "We caught a Starship, and now everything changes" (Space.com).

Musk inspects Ship 42

"I hugged ship 42 upon its return," Musk posted at 7:35 p.m. EST Saturday. After going over the heat shield on Sunday he wrote: "Overall, it looks great. The engines look great too. Ship 42 could probably be reflown with a few weeks of refurbishment. However, many small fixes are needed to achieve immediate reusability (like an airplane)." The ship rolled back to the production site on Sunday (Spaceflight Now).

What comes next

Starship V4 "will be ~70m long, which is roughly a 40% length increase and allows >60% more propellant load," with nine engines, including three more Raptor Vacuum engines, Musk posted at 8:33 p.m. EST Sunday; early Monday he wrote that "propellant cost per flight will drop below $500k and payload will exceed 200 tons." Still ahead is an orbital refueling demonstration needed before Starship can land astronauts on the Moon on Artemis IV, targeted for 2028, Space.com noted. Flight 16 could be the first Starship launch from the revamped pad at Kennedy Space Center, The New York Times reported.

Quick Hit

Jared Isaacman @NASAAdmin "We are looking forward to some of that on-orbit Starlink capability on Artemis III," the NASA administrator posted at 6:39 p.m. EST Friday, congratulating the SpaceX team on the launch.

III · Starlink

Twenty-six more V3 satellites, and gateways to match

Flight 15 doubled the number of next-generation Starlink satellites in orbit; Samsung is reported to be lining up a U.S. source for the laser-link chip, and Lufthansa sets its first Starlink long-haul flight.

"Today's Starlink payload will add up to 26 Terabits of capacity to the constellation," Starlink posted at 6:28 p.m. EST Friday. SpaceX made contact with every satellite, and they "are already beginning to raise their orbits to join the Starlink constellation," the company said. "Future Starship flights will carry over 50 Tb/s of Starlink bandwidth to orbit," Elon Musk wrote, and he added on Saturday that "there will ultimately be over 100k SpaceX communications satellites, most of which will be V4 or V5. Good chance that this system carries about half of global traffic" (post).

Before Friday, SpaceX had only 26 V3 satellites in orbit, PCMag noted. On Thursday, the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) granted licenses for 36 next-generation "quadband" gateway stations, each designed with 40 antennas to deliver gigabit-speed service through the V3 satellites, finding they "will not cause unacceptable interference" to other networks after Iridium had objected. "I think we'll need a few hundred V3 satellites for people to really feel the impact," SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell said on last month's earnings call.

Samsung to make Starlink's laser chip in Texas

Samsung Electronics plans to produce STMicroelectronics' STM32V8 microcontroller, which SpaceX uses in Starlink's laser communication system, at its Austin fab as part of a $1.9 billion expansion targeted to start operating by 2030, the Seoul Economic Daily reported Sunday, citing industry sources and the U.S. Commerce Department. Samsung said it cannot confirm contracts with individual customers.

Lufthansa's first Starlink long-haul flight

Lufthansa's first Airbus A350-1000, delivered Nov. 10, will be "the first Lufthansa Group long-haul aircraft equipped with the new Lufthansa Group Wi-Fi powered by Starlink" when it flies from Munich to Tokyo Haneda on Dec. 3, Runway Girl Network reported.

Quick Hit

United Airlines Starlink Wi-Fi is now on more than half of United's daily flights, with nearly 64% expected by the end of 2026 and every installation done by the end of 2027, the airline said.

IV · The Competition

Vulcan fails with 40 Amazon Leo satellites

United Launch Alliance's first total mission failure sets back Amazon's Starlink rival, which also has 13 Falcon 9 launches on order.

A Vulcan rocket launched at 12:40 p.m. EST Sunday from Cape Canaveral "suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit," United Launch Alliance (ULA) said, the first outright failure since the Boeing–Lockheed Martin venture was formed in 2006, Spaceflight Now reported. Something went wrong during the Centaur upper stage's burn; the cause is not yet known. It was Vulcan's fifth flight and its first in nine months.

The flight was the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon bought for Leo, Spaceflight Now noted, which is designed to compete head to head with Starlink; Amazon has also booked 24 launches on Blue Origin's New Glenn, 24 on Ariane 6 and 13 on SpaceX's Falcon 9, and needs more than 3,300 satellites for the constellation.

V · AI & Compute

Grok drops default watermarks; Citi bets on the AI segment

The SpaceXSI application programming interface (API) no longer watermarks text by default, and Citi raised its forecasts mainly for the Super Intelligence segment.

"Text watermarking is now off by default in the SpaceXSI API," the account @techdevnotes posted at 5:33 p.m. EST Friday. Elon Musk confirmed it on Saturday: "Text watermarking with Grok is only on where required by law or requested by the user." Earlier Friday, the Grok Bot account announced that the agent "can now join meetings. Ask it questions, assign it work, or have it keep track of what your team decides."

Citi raises its target to $280

Citi raised its SPCX price target to $280 from $240 after attending Flight 15 at Starbase, calling the ship catch a key milestone in a multi-year outlook that projects potential upside toward $900 or more, Investing.com reported Monday. It raised near-term revenue and adjusted earnings estimates, mainly for the Super Intelligence segment, citing "greater conviction" after talks with company representatives.
