The Morning Picture

A 9.1% drop as the first lock-up opens

SPCX fell 9.14% to $144.75 on Thursday, on 195 million shares, about twice its 30-session average, giving back all of Wednesday's 7.79% gain and more. The slide "followed profit-taking and a share unlock," The Motley Fool wrote; under the prospectus, up to 1.3 billion locked-up Class A shares could be sold from Thursday, with up to 328.4 million more from Saturday. The Nasdaq 100 lost 2.90% and the S&P 500 1.21%.

After the close, SpaceX filed its third-quarter 10-Q, which puts the Tesla talks in writing: "our board of directors has formed a special committee to evaluate a potential combination between SpaceX and Tesla, Inc.," and "No determination by our special committee has been made." Starlink 15-15, scrubbed a third straight day on Thursday, tries again this evening.

Today · NASA

Artemis update at 2 p.m.

Fri Oct 23 · 2:00 p.m. EDT · NASA media teleconference

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman and other agency leaders will discuss the status of upcoming Moon missions and efforts to accelerate the Artemis campaign, according to a NASA advisory. NASA Artemis announced the briefing on X Thursday.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Starlink 15-15 scrubs in the final minute

A third straight stand-down at Vandenberg, this time after fueling; the next try is Friday evening.

"Range weather went red," NASASpaceflight posted at 6:52 p.m. EDT Thursday. "This time, teams got through the fueling process and called off a launch attempt in the final minute. The scrub was called today due to poor weather at the launch pad," Spaceflight Now wrote four minutes later. At 7:21 p.m. SpaceX posted: "Due to unfavorable weather, we are standing down from today's @Starlink launch. Next opportunity is no earlier than Friday, October 23."

The attempts on Tuesday (technical issue) and Wednesday (weather) also ended in scrubs. The Falcon 9 carries 27 Starlink V2 Mini satellites on booster B1063's 36th flight, with a landing planned on the drone ship Of Course I Still Love You. Friday's window opens at 5:30 p.m. EDT, according to Spaceflight Now; Launch Library 2 lists 6:28:53 p.m. with a "Go" status.

Quick Hit

Booster home. Falcon 9 booster B1085, on its 19th flight, returned to Port Canaveral on Thursday, NASASpaceflight posted. Launch Library 2 lists no other Florida Falcon flight until Cygnus NG-25, targeted for late November.

Starship: two flows at once

"SpaceX is working on dual flows for its upcoming Starship missions, with Flight 15 set for mid-November from Pad 2. This is ahead of Flight 16's vehicles, which are expected to head to KSC for a launch from 39A," NASASpaceflight posted at 11:08 p.m. EDT Thursday. Flight 16 would be Starship's first launch from Florida, with Ship 43 and Booster 23 (NASASpaceflight).

At Starbase, the site "should be seeing road closures for Booster 22 to the pad and Static Fire testing soon," NASASpaceflight posted Thursday morning. That evening, a reposted update reported a deluge test at Pad 2 "following a couple hours of BQD and holddown" activity, as testing of Ship 39 continued at the Massey's site. Neither SpaceX nor the FAA has announced a launch date.

III · Starlink

Looking for 80,000 cell sites

SpaceX is in talks with tower owners for a ground network for Starlink Mobile.

SpaceX is seeking access to at least 80,000 U.S. cell sites for a wireless network and is in talks with Crown Castle, Bloomberg News reported Thursday, in deals that could reach into the billions of dollars. "You can assume that we're talking to all parties," Crown Castle's chief executive said; American Tower declined to comment (Reuters). On Tuesday's earnings call, Musk said the effort "will pave the way for Starlink Mobile to become a major mobile carrier in the US," Bloomberg noted.

The 10-Q counts 13.6 million Starlink consumer subscribers at September 30, up from 7.2 million a year earlier. Third-quarter revenue rose 69.5%, driven by 88.9% subscriber growth and a 16.9% drop in consumer revenue per user "as we expanded internationally and added lower-priced service plans," along with more aircraft activations.

Quick Hit

Spending. SpaceX spent $48.07 billion on property, plant and equipment in the first nine months, against $13.85 billion a year earlier, and ended September with $51.29 billion in cash plus $38.56 billion in marketable securities, the 10-Q shows.

IV · The Moon

"Way too high": 22 refuelings vs. 6 to 8

A NASA safety panel heard a new estimate for Starship's lunar landing; Musk disputed it.

The latest estimate for refuelings needed to support a Starship landing on the Moon is "around 22" launches, member Charlie Precourt said at Thursday's meeting of NASA's Aerospace Safety Advisory Panel, Ars Technica's Eric Berger posted at 2:43 p.m. EDT. "That's way too high. Actual number will be more like 6 to 8," Musk replied at 5:12 p.m. (Ars Technica). The panel also discussed concerns about Artemis IV and future access to low Earth orbit, SpacePolicyOnline reported.

Boeing's Starliner-1 mission is no earlier than mid-January, the panel said, Jeff Foust posted. If the space station's life is extended from 2030 to 2032, the U.S. Deorbit Vehicle, a modified Dragon, "would launch on a non-Falcon rocket," Berger posted.
