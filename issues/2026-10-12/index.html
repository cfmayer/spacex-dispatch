The Morning Picture

The SDA satellites fly, and Musk puts a ship catch on Starship's next flight

A Falcon 9 finally launched 21 Space Development Agency (SDA) data-relay satellites from California early Saturday, five days after an automatic abort, and its booster landed back at Vandenberg. Two more Falcon 9s are lined up: Starlink 15-25 on Monday evening, after slipping from Saturday and Sunday, and the CRS-35 cargo Dragon to the International Space Station (ISS) at 6:33 a.m. EDT Tuesday.

Speaking by video at a Tesla fan event in Texas on Saturday, Elon Musk said Starship Flight 15, "hopefully" four to six weeks away, will try to catch the ship with the launch tower. Starlink won a full operating license in Venezuela and offered free service in quake-hit Panama, while Musk escalated his fight with Mukesh Ambani over India. SPCX rose 1.25% Friday to $162.57 as Barclays started coverage at Overweight. SpaceX has filed nothing new with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) since Friday morning.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Third time lucky for the SDA, and cargo Dragon is next

After an abort and a week of day-by-day delays, Falcon 9 delivered the fourth Tranche 1 Transport Layer batch. CRS-35 is set for Tuesday morning from Florida.

T1TL-A in orbit

Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4E (SLC-4E) at Vandenberg Space Force Base at 3:39 a.m. EDT Saturday with 21 Northrop Grumman-built satellites for the SDA's Tranche 1 Transport Layer, Spaceflight Now reported. Booster B1103, on its sixth flight, landed at Landing Zone 4 about 7.5 minutes later, the 668th Falcon booster landing.

An October 5 attempt had stopped with 58 seconds left after the onboard computers flagged "some off-nominal data on Stage Two." SpaceX lowered the rocket for troubleshooting, then pushed the launch back a day at a time. "Additional time throughout the week allowed for double checks on vehicle systems and range deconfliction," SpaceX launch commentator Atticus Vadera said; Spaceflight Now said the range conflict is believed to have been a missile test from Vandenberg on Friday.

It was the fourth launch of production satellites for the SDA's Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture. Tranche 1 is to total 126 Transport Layer and 28 Tracking Layer satellites and begin operating for the Defense Department in 2027.

CRS-35 on Tuesday

NASA's 35th Commercial Resupply Services flight is scheduled for 6:33 a.m. EDT Tuesday, October 13, from Space Launch Complex 40 (SLC-40) at Cape Canaveral, per Spaceflight Now's schedule and Launch Library 2. Cargo Dragon C211, on its fourth flight, carries more than 6,300 pounds of science and supplies, including the last two ISS Roll-Out Solar Arrays, and is due to dock at the zenith port of the Harmony module on Thursday, October 15. Booster B1096 flies for the eighth time.

Starship status: a ship catch on Flight 15

Starship last flew on September 28. Flight 14 reached orbit and deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, though one engine shut down early. At the X Takeover event at Giga Texas on Saturday, Musk said the engine-out was "basically a failure in the flight computer software or engine computer software" and the heat shield held up, TSN Media reported from the interview:

"We don't know the exact launch date, but probably about four weeks or so. Four to six weeks is about right. Probably launches a little bit before Thanksgiving is my guess."
Elon Musk · X Takeover interview, Saturday, via TSN Media

"We catch the booster, which we've already proven … but we're going to try to catch the ship, which is a super big deal. And if we do so, that'll be the first time that there's been full recovery of an orbital class rocket," he said. SpaceX has published no Flight 15 date or mission plan.

Hardware is the other clock. Ship 42 had all six engines removed and went back to the Massey's test site for a third round of cryogenic proof testing, possibly after internal changes aimed at the Raptor Vacuum engine-outs seen on Flights 12 and 14, NASASpaceflight reported Friday; Booster 22 could static-fire as early as late this week. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) license covers the Flight 14 profile, and a changed mission such as a ship catch "could take a bit to get regulatory approval," NASASpaceflight wrote. Ship 40, from Flight 13, returned to Texas after 75 days at sea.

III · Starlink

A license in Caracas, free service in Panama, a standoff in New Delhi

Starlink's weekend ran from a new market to disaster relief to a political fight, and Ukraine's reliance on the network was back in the news.

Venezuela makes it official

Venezuela's telecom regulator, Conatel, approved a general operating license and seven Ku-band concessions for Starlink, letting it operate through its local company, SpaceX VZ, S.C.A., rather than on temporary permits, Drive Tesla reported, citing a Friday government announcement. Starlink handed out more than 1,600 terminals after the June 24 earthquakes in La Guaira. SpaceX has not said when sales start or what service will cost.

Panama earthquake

After a magnitude 7.7 earthquake (U.S. Geological Survey) struck central Panama on Friday, Starlink said new and existing customers in affected areas will get free service through November 10 and that it is working with emergency agencies, Drive Tesla reported. Carrier +Móvil opened Starlink Mobile satellite texting to all its customers from Friday through Sunday.

India: "Prime Minister Ambani"

Musk kept up daily posts on Starlink's stalled Indian start. On Friday he addressed Reliance chairman Mukesh Ambani as "Dear Prime Minister Ambani" and asked whether he would "consider allowing Starlink to compete?", Mint reported. Communications Minister Jyotiraditya Scindia replied that "India does not allow monopoly in any sector": Starlink, Jio Satcom and Eutelsat OneWeb all hold licences and can start once spectrum charges are set and each meets security conditions, the Hindustan Times reported. On Sunday India time Musk wrote that "if Starlink is too expensive in India, no one will buy it, so SpaceX will need to make pricing affordable" (The Hindu).

Quick Hit

Ukraine. After Musk backed Trump's call for a new Ukrainian president, presidential adviser Serhii Beskrestnov said SpaceX will not cut off Starlink because Ukraine brings it significant revenue (Euromaidan Press; bne IntelliNews).

IV · AI & Compute

Grok Bot learns to shop; Musk on why SpaceX bought Cursor

A new agent capability, and the X Takeover interview touched on AI and data centers in space.

"To buy stuff, you can just take a picture of your credit card, drop it in the chat and Grok @Bot will scour the Internet for the best deal on the item you want and order it," Musk posted Saturday; he added that Grok bought LEGO for him from LEGO's website, TeslaNorth reported, noting that he did not describe availability or spending safeguards.

In the X Takeover interview, Musk explained SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor as a source of AI training data and talent and discussed AI and data centers in space, according to the CEOInterviews summary of the 41-minute conversation.
