The Morning Picture

A $40 billion lease for GPUs

SpaceX has signed a binding commitment letter for a master finance lease of about $40 billion with an entity managed by Apollo, under which the lessor buys pods of graphics processing units (GPUs) and leases them to SpaceX, according to an 8-K accepted by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) at 8:00 a.m. EDT Monday. The letter was signed on Saturday, October 24. Each lease schedule runs about five to five and a quarter years, with rent paid quarterly in advance: one-twentieth of the financed cost plus a fixed annual rate and costs. Closing is expected before the end of the year, and schedules begin in the first half of 2027 as the hardware is delivered.

Elon Musk had previewed the deal on Sunday. "SpaceX closed $40B of debt for GPUs, but we're investment grade and have Starlink & launch revenue," he posted at 3:11 p.m. EDT, and half an hour later added that it is "primarily callable debt for GPU purchases that will arrive in Q1 and Q2. Not a major factor this quarter." CNBC reported the filing Monday morning. SPCX rose 1.89% on Friday to $147.49.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Starlink 15-15 flies on the fourth try

Falcon 9 lifted off from Vandenberg Friday evening after three scrubs, and Super Heavy Booster 22 fired all 33 engines on Sunday.

Falcon 9 lifted off from Vandenberg Space Force Base at 6:48 p.m. EDT Friday with Starlink 15-15, "After three previous scrubs," Spaceflight Now posted. The booster, B1063, was making its 36th flight (NASASpaceflight). At 7:51 p.m. EDT SpaceX posted: "Deployment of 27 @Starlink satellites confirmed."

Booster 22's static fire

At Starbase, Texas, Booster 22 was lifted onto the launch mount at Pad 2 on Saturday (NASASpaceflight) and fired its engines at 9:22 a.m. EDT Sunday, Spaceflight News reported. "Full duration 33-engine static fire for the Super Heavy booster preparing for Flight 15," SpaceX posted at 3:49 p.m. EDT. The booster was lifted back off the pad after midnight, while Ship 43, assigned to Flight 16, returned from the Massey's test site and Ship 42, for Flight 15, took its place, NASASpaceflight posted.

Quick Hit

Florida debut. Flight 16 is "set to be Starship's Florida debut," NASASpaceflight posted Sunday, with fresh flyover photos of the launch facilities.

Starship: Raptor fix, catches planned

NASA's Artemis program manager, Jeremy Parsons, said at Friday's briefing that SpaceX has found the root cause of the Raptor engine relight failures and that an improved Raptor flies on Flight 15, Spaceflight News reported. Flight 15 is probably in November, both vehicles have been cryo-proofed, and the flight will likely attempt booster and ship catches. A photo from the Starfactory shown at the event "confirms upgraded Raptors for Flight 15," NASASpaceflight posted.

Sunday's test closed the beach and State Highway 4 under a mayor's order, with Monday as a backup day (Spaceflight News). SpaceX has not announced a launch date.

III · Starlink

easyJet signs on; Ukraine's drones speed up again

A second European airline brand, schools in Bolivia, and a weekend fight over Starlink's terms of service.

Airlines

easyJet will offer onboard connectivity for the first time, with Starlink installed across its A320, A320neo and A321neo fleet from the first half of 2027 and the rollout finished by early 2028, the airline announced Monday. Starlink posted at 8:26 a.m. EDT that passengers will be able to "work, stream, or stay in touch without interruption." Aer Lingus has finished installing free Starlink Wi-Fi on its long-haul fleet of A330s and A321LR/XLRs, covering all of its U.S. routes, One Mile at a Time reported. "A cunning move by Aer Lingus," Musk posted Sunday.

Ukraine

Ukraine's Defense Ministry said Saturday that Starlink and Starshield speeds had been restored and that the connection "remains stable" on equipment moving above 400 kilometers per hour, CNBC reported. Since October 14, Ukrainian officials had accused SpaceX of speed limits on Starshield that cut off drones flying faster than 100 miles per hour. President Donald Trump confirmed a report that he had asked Musk to ease the restrictions: "I have spoken to Elon Musk."

"Ukraine military violated Starlink terms of service and used it for offensive drone strikes to kill people. That loophole was closed last week," Musk wrote (CNBC). "Starshield, a different system, is controlled by the US government. What they decide to do is up to them," he posted, and later: "We simply enforced our terms of service when they were bypassed. There has been no change to our terms of service" (post). Lawmakers from both parties have called for a formal probe of the changes (CNBC).

Quick Hit

Bolivia's schools. President Rodrigo Paz signed agreements with 85 municipalities to install Starlink antennas in schools, with a national target of about 4,700 antennas, Correo del Sur reported. "Starlink in Bolivia!" Musk posted.

Starlink V3, "which only just started launching, will have ~100 times more bandwidth than the current V2 system," Musk posted Saturday, replying to a post that put Starlink's subscribers at 13.6 million.

IV · The Moon

Artemis III: Blue Moon first, then Starship

NASA laid out a 14-day Earth-orbit test mission for 2027, with Orion docking to both companies' lander test articles.

Artemis III will send NASA astronauts Randy Bresnik, Frank Rubio and Andre Douglas and the European Space Agency's Luca Parmitano into low Earth orbit in 2027 to practice with test versions of the Blue Origin and SpaceX landers, NASA said Friday. Blue Origin launches a Blue Moon Mark 2 test article first; Orion docks to it for about two days of crewed checks. SpaceX then launches a Starship Version 3 test article from Florida, which will "carry an added docking system mounted at the forward end." Orion docks to it for up to two hours, and "Crew will not enter Starship during this test."

The Starship docking comes on flight day eight, NASASpaceflight posted from the briefing, where NASA also said two Starlink terminals are installed on the Artemis III Orion (post). The first crewed landing is planned for Artemis IV in 2028 (NASA). Asked how many tanker flights a Starship landing needs, Parsons said NASA was not tracking as high as 22 and pointed to SpaceX's estimate of six to eight (Spaceflight News). "Back to the Moon," Musk posted.

V · AI & Compute

"Guppy-level death" for neoclouds

Musk argued that debt markets have closed to most GPU renters, and said SpaceX will not buy them.

"As of the past few weeks, debt markets have almost entirely shut off access to money for any company that doesn't have substantial revenue independent of AI data centers," Musk posted Sunday. "That means the 200+ neoclouds are going to see guppy-level death and consolidation. In a separate post he wrote: "SpaceX is not buying any neoclouds. Pointless."

"I think we're going to see a lot of stranded compute in partially complete data centers," he wrote Saturday night, and on demand: "Agentic demand is growing faster than I've ever seen anything grow" (post). On how to value GPUs, he posted that "Accounting depreciation is irrelevant," adding, "Delivering on projected cash flow is what matters."
