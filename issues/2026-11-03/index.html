The Morning Picture

"Boost it": Isaacman on Hubble's future

"Hubble, you can't let it have an uncontrolled re-entry. That would not be the right thing to do. If you're going to spend money to go up there and do something, you might as well boost it, get as much science as you possibly can out of it," NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman told NASASpaceflight in an interview published Tuesday.

NASA issued a Request for Information on Oct. 29 asking companies whether they could boost Hubble to a higher orbit, service it to enhance its capabilities, or carry out a controlled re-entry or disposal, NASA said. Responses are due by 3 p.m. EST on Monday, Nov. 30. Hubble has no propulsion of its own and is likely to re-enter in 2033 or 2034, so a mission would need to fly by 2032, the agency said.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

USSF-432 slips to Friday

The Space Force mission has moved twice since Monday; SpaceX now targets a four-hour window Friday evening from Vandenberg.

SpaceX is targeting Friday, Nov. 6, for a Falcon 9 launch of the USSF-432 mission from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California, with a four-hour window opening at 7:46 p.m. EST, SpaceX said. The backup is Saturday at 7:32 p.m. EST. Monday's issue had listed Tuesday at 8:28 p.m. EST; no reason has been given for the moves.

The first-stage booster will fly for the eighth time after launching GPS III-8 and six Starlink missions, and will land on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You in the Pacific (SpaceX). Residents of Santa Barbara, San Luis Obispo and Ventura counties may hear one or more sonic booms.

Hubble: from Polaris to Starship

A Polaris Program proposal led by Isaacman to service Hubble was studied, and NASA did not pursue it in 2024, NASASpaceflight noted. Isaacman also pointed to NASA's unsuccessful attempt to reboost the Swift telescope: "That was absolutely a risk worth taking."

"I wouldn't rule out the possibility that well into the future somebody could go and get it and bring it back and put it in a museum," Isaacman said. Hubble now runs in single-gyro mode, which has cut observation time by about 12%, according to NASASpaceflight.

"Hubble still has important science ahead of it, and we should be looking at every reasonable opportunity to keep it delivering for as long as possible," Isaacman said in the NASA announcement. The final shuttle servicing mission, STS-125, flew in May 2009.

III · Starlink

Boost is no shortcut, analysts say

Boost's network deals with AT&T and T-Mobile would not simply pass to SpaceX, analysts said.

"SpaceX cannot simply acquire Boost Mobile and inherit its MVNO agreements with AT&T and T-Mobile," LightShed Partners analysts Walt Piecyk and Joe Galone wrote, as quoted by Light Reading on Tuesday, a day after The Washington Post reported talks between SpaceX and EchoStar. An MVNO (mobile virtual network operator) resells another carrier's network. Boost's deal with T-Mobile expires on July 1, 2027, MoffettNathanson analyst Craig Moffett noted.

EchoStar's network services agreement with AT&T includes limits on any "restricted party," and AT&T can end it with a two-year transition, Light Reading reported from the filings. New Street Research analyst David Barden called the filings' language that a deal "may need consent" "a gross understatement," and said SpaceX's plan A is its own terrestrial network on its 800MHz spectrum, plus an MVNO agreement.

"For the first time, there is an entity capable of scaring investors in the traditional big three," Blair Levin, a former Federal Communications Commission chief of staff, told Axios. The Post reported that Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile have all rebuffed Musk's efforts to gain access to their networks, according to Axios. Boost stores already sell Starlink service (Light Reading).

A rescue at 12,000 feet

"Starlink saves lives," Musk posted at 8:49 a.m. EST Monday about a Colorado rescue. A 37-year-old solo hiker on the Four Pass Loop texted "Please help me" to Pitkin County dispatchers at about 8 p.m. on Oct. 29, and five Crested Butte Search and Rescue volunteers hiked through the night "carrying a Starlink satellite communication system that allowed them to remain in contact with their mission coordinator," Unofficial Networks reported.

The rescuers reached her near Frigid Air Pass at about 6 a.m. in snow up to four feet deep, and a Colorado Army National Guard Black Hawk hoisted her out at about 9:15 a.m. Oct. 30. She was flown to the UCHealth Burn and Frostbite Center in Aurora (Unofficial Networks).

IV · AI & Compute

"Much easier to cool in space"

Musk made the case for radiators in orbit; two more Colossus 2 sites aim to open by December.

"Yeah, much easier to cool in space," Musk posted at 10:43 p.m. EST Monday. "~1300 W/m^2 is easily achievable for an aluminum radiator pointed away from the Sun, as it is mostly just seeing cosmic background radiation at ~3 degrees above absolute zero! Solar panels generate ~250 W/m^2, so you should expect them to have ~5 times the area of the radiator. Most of the mass of the satellite is solar panels."

He was replying to investor Shaun Maguire, who wrote that "99.9999% of that challenge is Starship." Earlier, at 10:08 a.m., Musk wrote: "Future super intelligence compute will obviously be overwhelmingly in space. By 'overwhelmingly', I mean that it will round up to 100%!"

Colossus 2: Gun Range and Spam

The two new Colossus 2 sites in Southaven, Mississippi, will hold about 420,000 Nvidia GB300 chips between them, DCD reported. Gun Range, a former police and fire training center, will have two single-story buildings on 55.9 acres for AI training; Spam, a former warehouse for Hormel products, is a 53-acre site for inference. Musk said Gun Range is tracking to be operational by the end of November and Spam in December.

The expansion comes after Mississippi's Department of Environmental Quality said construction at SpaceXAI sites began before stormwater permit coverage and sought civil penalties of up to $25,000 a day, with preliminary exposure of about $6.1 million across three sites, DCD wrote. A judge has also rejected a Justice Department attempt to block an NAACP lawsuit over the company's gas turbines in Southaven.
