The Morning Picture

SpaceX buys its way into the phone business, and carrier stocks take the hit

After the market closed on Thursday, SpaceX said it will buy Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz spectrum portfolio, up to 14 megahertz of paired low-band airwaves that it says will let Starlink Mobile "become a major mobile carrier in the US." The price was not disclosed; The Wall Street Journal reported about $8 billion in cash. The deal still needs approval from the Federal Communications Commission (FCC). By Friday morning, shares of AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile were down 5.5% to 7.4% before the open, Reuters reported.

The news came too late to help SPCX, which fell 4.19% to $160.57 during Thursday's session, its second straight loss, before rising about 2.5% in extended trading. Up to 328.4 million more Class A shares become free to sell from Friday under the IPO lock-up schedule. Crew-12 is home after Thursday's splashdown. Next on the launch schedule: a Space Development Agency (SDA) mission early Saturday and the CRS-35 cargo flight on Tuesday. SpaceX has filed nothing new with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) since Thursday morning.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Crew-12 home; cargo Dragon rolls out this weekend

NASA and SpaceX reviewed the splashdown and set up Tuesday's station resupply flight; an SDA launch from California comes first, early Saturday.

A 237-day mission ends

The Dragon capsule Freedom splashed down off Los Angeles at 11:34 a.m. EDT Thursday. NASA said the crew's 237 days aboard the International Space Station (ISS) included research into medicine, quantum science and 3D printing with metal. The crew of NASA astronauts Jessica Meir and Jack Hathaway, European Space Agency (ESA) astronaut Sophie Adenot and Roscosmos cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev was lifted aboard the recovery ship Shannon.

"I am always relieved, grateful and proud when we complete a crew mission— welcome home to Mother Earth Crew 12!"
Gwynne Shotwell, President and COO · @Gwynne_Shotwell · post, Thu 5:05 p.m. EDT

Next cargo run: Tuesday

At a post-splashdown teleconference, Lee Echerd, a senior mission manager at SpaceX, said Falcon 9 and Dragon will roll out of the hangar at Space Launch Complex 40 this weekend, with the final launch readiness review on Monday, Spaceflight Now reported. The 35th Commercial Resupply Services mission (CRS-35) is set for 6:33 a.m. EDT Tuesday, October 13, with a backup at 6:08 a.m. EDT Wednesday. Dragon would dock at about 11:00 a.m. EDT Thursday, October 15, SpaceX said. It carries more than 6,300 pounds, according to NASA, including the last two ISS Roll-Out Solar Arrays and an empty container that will bring home a failed Canadarm2 joint removed during a June 30 spacewalk (Spaceflight Now).

SDA launch set for Saturday

Falcon 9 is due to launch 21 Northrop Grumman-built satellites for the SDA's Tranche 1 Transport Layer at 3:29 a.m. EDT Saturday from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. It is the fourth of six such missions. SpaceX moved it on Wednesday from Friday, saying "additional time allows for double checks on vehicle systems and range deconfliction." Friday morning's Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) operations plan advisory lists a 3:29 to 4:34 a.m. EDT window Saturday and a backup Sunday.

Quick Hit

Dragon fleet. Echerd said the capsule Resilience, which flew Inspiration4, Polaris Dawn and Fram2, stays in its free-flier configuration, while the other Crew Dragons remain in service for now (Spaceflight Now). NASA's Bill Spetch said a Cygnus mission is planned toward the end of the year (Spaceflight Now).

Starship status

Starship last flew on September 28. Flight 14 was the first Starship flight to place satellites, a batch of Starlink V3 spacecraft, into a stable low Earth orbit (Launch Library 2), and the ship splashed down north of Hawaii (Spaceflight Now).

An unofficial schedule from NASASpaceflight, posted Thursday, lists Flight 15 from Starbase Pad 2 no earlier than November 8 and Flight 16, potentially from Launch Complex 39A in Florida, no earlier than December 10. No new FAA license action has been published; comments on Starship at Space Launch Complex 37 close Wednesday (Section VI).

III · Starlink

"A major mobile carrier in the US"

SpaceX agreed to buy nationwide low-band spectrum, the piece it says Starlink Mobile was missing to work indoors. The FCC still has to sign off.

$2.9BCash T-Mobile received from Grain for the same licenses in August, plus 600 MHz spectrum
15,000Next-generation direct-to-cell satellites the FCC approved this week

What SpaceX is buying

SpaceX announced the agreement at 4:19 p.m. EDT Thursday. In a company update, it said the portfolio of up to 14 megahertz of paired spectrum "addresses one of the key remaining technical gaps." Once the FCC approves, Starlink Mobile plans to combine its satellites with "an advanced terrestrial deployment." The 2 GHz band SpaceX bought from EchoStar would carry high-bandwidth capacity, while the 800 MHz layer is meant to get through walls and reach phones inside buildings. "Critically, most existing mobile devices already support this underused band," SpaceX said.

"With this new low-band spectrum and our Gen2 constellation, Starlink Mobile can now become the first network operator to deploy both satellite and terrestrial spectrum. One network that provides Americans with a reliable service indoors, outdoors, in cellular dead zones, and everywhere in between."
SpaceX · update, Thursday, October 8

Grain Management, a Washington, D.C., digital-infrastructure investor, said SpaceX will acquire 100% of the portfolio, subject to FCC approval and customary closing conditions. Grain bought the licenses from T-Mobile in August for $2.9 billion in cash plus its own 600 MHz licenses, MarketWatch reported. Grain said the sale advances the FCC's July 2026 order approving that purchase, which set up a competitive selection process and "a conditional pathway" for direct-to-device service. Neither side disclosed a price; Reuters, citing The Wall Street Journal, put it at about $8 billion in cash.

"The last critical piece"

Elon Musk (@elonmusk) wrote: "This is the last critical piece of the spectrum puzzle needed for SpaceX to provide complete phone coverage in America." Replying to investor Chamath Palihapitiya, who called it "a very big deal," Musk added: "To the casual observer, this won't seem like much. To those who understand the spectrum wars, it's an earthquake." Michael Nicolls (@michaelnicollsx), vice president of Starlink engineering, posted "Big day for @Starlink Mobile…"

The same company update recapped Tuesday's FCC approval of the Gen2 direct-to-cell constellation: 15,000 satellites optimized for 2 GHz spectrum, which SpaceX says will deliver more than 100 times the bandwidth of the current generation, plus the full set of Ka-, V-, E- and W-band backhaul frequencies used by Starlink's V3 broadband satellites.

India: "We are ready to serve in India, once India is ready"

India's Ministry of Communications called claims that its satellite rules are applied in a discriminatory way "baseless and misconceived." It said Starlink, Eutelsat OneWeb and Jio Satellite Communications all hold licenses, all face security assessments now under way, and sit at "broadly the same regulatory stage," Hindustan Times reported. Starlink still needs security clearance and a spectrum assignment before it can sell service. At the India Mobile Congress, Starlink vice president Lauren Dreyer said the company has built 20 gateway sites with hundreds of antennas around India's security rules: "We are ready to serve in India, once India is ready."

Jio, owned by Mukesh Ambani's Reliance, is reported to be planning about 1,600 low Earth orbit satellites and 23 ground stations, CNBC noted, citing local media. Akash Ambani, chairman of Reliance Jio Infocomm, told the same conference that Jio is developing its own satellite technology (BBC). Musk kept up the pressure Thursday afternoon, quoting a post that argued India needs Starlink as an emergency backup: "Starlink saves lives during natural disasters."

IV · The Carrier Fight

Phone stocks slide on both sides of the Atlantic

Carriers fell, tower owners rose, and analysts argued over how fast a satellite-first network can take customers.

AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile each fell more than 7% after hours on Thursday (MarketWatch). On Friday morning they were down 5.5% to 7.4% before the open, and the STOXX Europe 600 telecommunications index lost more than 2%, its lowest since January 30, Reuters reported. Deutsche Telekom fell 6.5%, Vodafone 4.1%, Telefónica 3.9% and Orange 2.3%.

"We believe the Grain transaction is a clear sign that SpaceX is going to be a more aggressive acquirer of spectrum."
Morgan Stanley analysts, via Reuters

Morgan Stanley added that any threat to established carriers will likely build gradually, starting in rural markets. Tim Farrar of TMF Associates told CNBC: "This adds fuel to the fire in the battle between SpaceX and the mobile operators. But it's still a very limited amount of spectrum and to get reliable building penetration in urban areas SpaceX would have to deploy towers on the ground."

Quick Hit

Tower owners rallied. American Tower, Crown Castle and SBA Communications rose 6.7% to 8.3% Friday morning; Morgan Stanley called the deal "incrementally constructive" for them, since even a satellite-centric network needs towers, rooftops and small cells (Reuters).

A relationship already fraying

Last week AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon launched a joint venture to pool spectrum for satellite coverage beyond their towers, run by interim CEO Paul Roth, and the three have said they do not plan to sign wholesale (MVNO) deals with SpaceX, Light Reading reported. T-Mobile, Starlink's U.S. direct-to-cell partner, recently removed Starlink references from its T-Satellite promotions (CNBC). In August, SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell said she expects Starlink to "acquire quite a few of their customers because I think our service will be better" (MarketWatch).

Starlink had 12 million subscribers in 205 countries as of June 30, and its connectivity business earned $1.66 billion of operating income on $4.29 billion of revenue in the second quarter, CNBC noted. Deutsche Bank estimated 13.5 million subscribers at the end of September, 4.1 million of them in the U.S.

V · AI & Compute

Musk's biggest claim yet

No update on the reported $40 billion chip financing; SpaceXAI kept shipping Grok products and funding open-source work.

"This will sound super crazy, but I see a path to SpaceX being worth orders of magnitude more than the current Earth economy"
Elon Musk · @elonmusk · post, Thu 5:55 p.m. EDT

The reported talks with Apollo and banks on about $40 billion of debt to buy Nvidia chips were still weighing on the stock Thursday, TipRanks said. Neither company has confirmed them. On the model side, Musk wrote "Grok 4.7 ranks first in legal matters," quoting Artificial Analysis's updated Legal Agent Benchmark, which now includes a hallucination check. SpaceXAI's Grok Imagine released Video 1.5 Lite in its developer interface at $0.02 a second for 480p, $0.03 for 720p and $0.14 for 1080p.

Quick Hit

David Heinemeier Hansson @dhh, creator of Ruby on Rails and the Omarchy Linux setup, said SpaceXAI joined the Omacom Foundation as a founding corporate patron with $1.5 million in Grok tokens. SpaceXAI replied: "We are glad to support the Omarchy team."
