The Morning Picture

Musk: SpaceX and Tesla "probably" better together

Speaking by video from China at The New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday, Elon Musk said SpaceX and Tesla have "more and more areas of interaction or overlap," mostly in AI and robotics, and that the two would "probably" be better together, though any deal needs shareholder votes, CNBC reported. He also said Anthropic, which priced its initial public offering (IPO) at $83 a share on Wednesday and starts trading today, wants "an incremental 10 gigawatts" of SpaceX compute.

In Texas, Cameron County commissioners voted 3–2 against moving a 10-year tax break for SpaceX's $24 billion Starbase data center and gas plant to a hearing, and SpaceX warned of swings in local tax rates. The stock rose 1.27% to $143.50. The CRS-35 cargo Dragon's departure slipped again, to Friday, and USSF-317 now targets 11:28 p.m. EST tonight from California.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Dragon's trip home slips to Friday

Splashdown-zone weather holds the CRS-35 cargo ship at the station another day; NASA sets the next Cygnus launch on a Falcon 9 for Nov. 29.

SpaceX is "now targeting no earlier than Friday, November 20 for Dragon's departure" from the International Space Station (ISS), with teams "continuing to monitor weather at the splashdown locations," the company posted at 4:48 p.m. EST Wednesday. Pending weather, the capsule will undock from the Harmony module at 12:05 p.m. EST Friday, with NASA coverage from 11:45 a.m., and splash down off California no earlier than 5:04 p.m. EST Saturday, Nov. 21, NASA's updated advisory says. The Thursday attempt announced early Wednesday did not hold.

The crew kept packing in the meantime: NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney stowed crew blood samples and research samples kept in science freezers and incubators, and Anil Menon moved cargo from the Kibo laboratory into the capsule, NASA's station blog said. The return load includes 3D-bioprinted blood vessel samples, antibiotic-resistant bacteria, a circadian lighting system, a spacesuit for refurbishment and a failed wrist joint from the Canadarm2 robotic arm.

Cygnus rides a Falcon 9 on Nov. 29

NASA, Northrop Grumman and SpaceX are targeting no earlier than 10:47 a.m. EST Sunday, Nov. 29, for the next station cargo launch: a Cygnus XL with about 11,000 pounds of cargo on a Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, NASA said. Watkins and Delaney are to capture the spacecraft, named for former NASA astronaut Jim Lovell, with the Canadarm2 on Tuesday, Dec. 1; a prelaunch teleconference streams at noon EST Monday, Nov. 23. The station's account announced the date at 8:03 p.m. EST Wednesday.

"The last major piece of the puzzle"

"Catching the ship was the last major piece of the puzzle," Musk wrote at 8:04 p.m. EST Wednesday, quoting a clip about Saturday's first tower catch of a Starship upper stage. SpaceX posted a photo of "the Starbase tower team on location with the first caught Starship" at 11:56 a.m.

USSF-380 joins the list

USSF-380, the third Space Force launch from Vandenberg on the schedule within nine days, appeared Wednesday with a target of 11:53 p.m. EST Friday, Nov. 27, on a booster flying for the 27th time, Launch Library 2 shows, citing SpaceX's mission page.

Starship status

Flight 15 (Booster 22, Ship 42) launched from Starbase Pad 2 on Nov. 13, deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites and ended 22.5 hours later with the first catch of a ship on Nov. 14; the booster's boostback burn "ended early due to low fuel mass, with the root cause under investigation" (SpaceX). The FAA's Nov. 13 license revision covered that single flight (Seeking Alpha). No Flight 16 date has been announced; it could be the first Starship launch from LC-39A in Florida (The New York Times). The FAA's last virtual scoping meeting on more Starship launches from Starbase is Thursday (Federal Register).

III · Starlink

"Diversify away. Don't use Starlink."

Musk defends the line between Starlink and the military Starshield as Canada's prime minister argues for a backup, and previews rooftop cell sites for Starlink Mobile.

"From the beginning, I've said that Starlink is a civilian system. It is not meant to be used for offensive drone strikes that kill people," Musk said at DealBook. "If you want to do that, you need to do Starshield, which is a separate system that is controlled by the US government. But do not ask me as a private individual to make kill decisions." Asked whether Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy was lying when he said Starshield had been slowed, Musk said: "He is lying," adding that Zelenskyy "wants to use Starlink for offensive military operations," The Verge reported.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, also at the summit on Wednesday, said the Starshield speed limits in Ukraine show why Canada wants a sovereign or at least redundant alternative: "You can't unsee that once you've seen it." Ottawa and Brussels agreed last month to negotiate Canada's access to the Galileo navigation system and explore the IRIS2 secure-communications network, though competing with Starlink commercially is not the goal, The Logic reported. When the interviewer raised talk of diversifying away from Starlink, Musk replied, "Diversify away. Don't use Starlink," according to a transcript of the interview; he said "the same rules that have been in place for Starlink from the beginning are in place today" and "we've not turned off Starlink."

Rooftop "micro cell" sites for phones indoors

SpaceX will start launching its second-generation direct-to-cell constellation next year with "40 times the bandwidth" of the first, Musk said, and will use the 800 MHz spectrum it agreed to buy for "micro cell" sites that reach phones inside buildings, where a metal roof blocks the signal from space. "We have a design for this and it's going into production, I think, next year," he said of a unit "that you can just put on your roof like a Starlink dish," adding that he does not expect AT&T or Verizon to "go out of business" (transcript).

Starlink as Starship's paymaster

"These useful things we do with communications allow us to fund the development of advanced rockets like Starship," Musk told the summit, describing Starlink's second role as paying for rockets "capable of extending life as we know it" to self-sustaining cities on the Moon and Mars. He estimated that "hundreds of lives have been saved because Starlink was the only means of connectivity in a natural disaster," and said SpaceX files only defensive patents: "I encourage people to copy our rockets and to copy our satellites" (transcript).

Starlink Mobile goes live in Mongolia

"Starlink Mobile is now available in Mongolia" with carrier Mobicom, letting phones outside cell coverage connect to the satellites automatically for messaging apps and SMS, Starlink posted at 2:13 p.m. EST Wednesday. Mobicom's mobispace service started Nov. 16; customers who activate before Dec. 1 get the first month free, and it needs an open view of the sky and does not work near the border, Tesla North reported.

Quick Hit

Botswana next. Starlink Mobile is coming to Botswana with carrier BTC "early next year," covering places such as the Central Kalahari National Park, Starlink posted at 12:01 p.m. EST Wednesday.

IV · Starbase

County shelves the Starbase data-center tax break

Cameron County commissioners decline to schedule a hearing on a 10-year abatement for SpaceX's $24 billion compute campus and gas plant; SpaceX warns of tax-rate swings.

Cameron County commissioners voted 3–2 on Tuesday not to move forward with a public hearing on SpaceX's request for a 10-year, 100% tax abatement for its data center at Starbase, with County Judge Eddie Treviño Jr. and Commissioner Joey Lopez in favor, The Brownsville Herald reported. "As of right now with the court, it's not going to happen, so it's dead in the water," Lopez said; the hearing would have been on Dec. 29. Residents and groups including the South Texas Environmental Justice Network, Valley Interfaith and the Surfrider Foundation urged a no vote during the meeting's first hour.

The application lists a $24 billion natural gas-fired power plant and co-located "super compute facility" with closed-loop cooling and behind-the-meter power, a first-year taxable value of $13.8 billion, 500 new jobs and 5,500 peak construction jobs, with construction from this quarter to the second quarter of 2027, according to the Herald and KRGV. Without the abatement, SpaceX said in the application, it would "redirect current and/or future investment to other super compute facility sites outside Cameron County." It applied on Oct. 15 and revised the request on Nov. 9.

SpaceX: depreciation could swing tax rates

"As Texas communities consider major supercomputing investments, careful long-term tax planning is essential," SpaceX posted at 6:14 p.m. EST Wednesday. Under Texas's no-new-revenue tax framework, rising property values generally drive tax rates down, the company said, but computing equipment loses value quickly, which it argues could cause large swings in taxable values and future rates; the abatement would have given the county more predictable revenue, it said, according to the RGV Business Journal. Starbase has posted notice of a Dec. 9 hearing on a SpaceX property-tax abatement request for the project (MLQ.ai).

V · AI & Compute

Musk: Anthropic wants 10 more gigawatts

SpaceX's biggest compute customer prices its record IPO, Musk puts the compute build at half a gigawatt a month, and a Tesla merger gets a "probably yes."

Anthropic has told him "their sort of highest probability outcome is that they would like an incremental 10 gigawatts of compute from SpaceX. So that's a big number," Musk said at DealBook. He said he met many Anthropic employees before renting them compute and "no one I met at Anthropic set off my 'evil detector,'" and that the contracts can be ended on three to six months' notice, though he called that unlikely (transcript; Dealroom).

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, above its expected $70 to $78 range, and begins trading on Nasdaq as ANT on Thursday; selling every share would raise more than $68 billion, SiliconANGLE reported. Its filings commit up to $120.5 billion to SpaceX compute through 2029, cancelable by either side on 90 days' notice after the first quarter of 2027, The Motley Fool noted.

Half a gigawatt a month

SpaceX is "right now, in rough terms, bringing on about half a gigawatt per month of AI compute," roughly 220,000 Nvidia GB300 chips with their networking and cooling, and should pass a gigawatt a month by the end of next year, Musk told the iConnections TomorrowX Summit from Shenzhen, Druckfin reported. He said Wall Street estimates for the cost of 10 gigawatts are off "by a factor of two" and put his own cost at "maybe 60%" of consensus. He described the planned orbital compute satellite, "Star Mind," as essentially a large solar array and radiator around a compute module about a quarter the size of an Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 rack, linked to Starlink by laser.

Terafab: first test chip around June

"Looking for hardcore engineers to join Terafab!" Musk posted at 8:38 p.m. EST Wednesday, quoting his remarks that the research fab should make its first test chip "around June of next year," with volume production for phase one "towards the end of 2028," and that the plant could ultimately reach 100 million square feet.

A Tesla merger: "probably yes"

"You don't need them to be together, but would they be better together? I mean, probably yes," Musk said of SpaceX and Tesla, citing their AI software and chip design groups and his view that orbital compute "is really going to be where things scale." Deals "require shareholder votes, which may or may not succeed, at least in the case of Tesla," he said, and he "can't just drag" SpaceX's other shareholders into a non-economic merger. He said he should not receive Tesla stock option awards "simply for merging with SpaceX" (CNBC; transcript).

Quick Hit

Grok 4.8. The model "is currently several different model variants in beta. We're not yet sure which of the variants will go to wide release," Musk wrote at 8:28 p.m. EST Wednesday, replying to a clip in which he said the model is in its reinforcement-learning phase.
