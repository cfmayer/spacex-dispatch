The Morning Picture

A squeeze test at catch height

Booster 22, the first Super Heavy V3 booster meant to return to the launch site for a catch, rolled to the pad at Starbase and was held aloft by the tower's arms for a "suspended squeeze test at catch height," SpaceX posted Tuesday at 1:21 p.m. EST. Starship Flight 15 still targets Friday at 5:45 p.m. EST, pending regulatory approval, and airspace notices for the launch went up Wednesday morning.

Off the pad, SpaceX signed a deal with Germany's SINGULUS for a solar-cell production line to power its Starmind satellites, and the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) opened its review of SpaceX's purchase of nationwide 800 MHz spectrum. USSF-627 slipped a second time, to tonight, and SPCX rose 0.72%.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Booster 22 gets its squeeze; Flight 15 airspace notices go up

The pad is set for Friday's double-catch attempt, while two Space Force launches from California slip again.

Booster 22 "was used for final catch testing upon arrival, including a suspended squeeze test at catch height," SpaceX said on X, sharing four photos of the booster in the Mega Bay and in the grip of the tower's "chopstick" arms, Space.com reported. SpaceX has caught Super Heavy boosters three times but has never tried to catch a ship.

United States airspace notices to airmen (NOTAMs) for Friday's launch were published Wednesday morning, alongside a Cuban notice posted Tuesday, SpaceNotices reported at 8:03 a.m. EST. The Federal Aviation Administration's (FAA) operations advisory lists a launch window of 5:45 to 7:40 p.m. EST Friday and a ship reentry window of 3:56 to 7:14 p.m. EST Saturday, with backups a day later. SpaceX's mission page now shows a 75-minute window opening at 5:45 p.m. EST.

Quick Hit

Starbase. A temporary flight restriction up to 11,000 feet for a "high energy testing ground hazard" at the Starbase launch site, starting Nov. 12, has been published; it is not a launch or reentry restriction, SpaceNotices noted Tuesday.

Starship status

Starship reached orbit for the first time on Flight 14, launched Sept. 28, Space.com noted. On Flight 15, the booster is to be caught about six and a half minutes after liftoff; the ship is to deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites, orbit at about 275 kilometers for roughly 14 orbits and then try the first ship catch at Pad 2 more than 22 hours after launch, according to SpaceX. Three of the satellites carry cameras to scan Starship's heat shield. At Pad 1, the second of four sloped wall sections is being installed in the flame trench, NASASpaceflight posted. Flight 16 is expected to be the first Starship launch from Launch Complex 39A (LC-39A) in Florida, NASASpaceflight has reported.

USSF-627 slips again, to tonight

SpaceX moved the USSF-627 launch for the U.S. Space Force a second time, from Tuesday at 9:49 p.m. EST to Wednesday, Nov. 11, with a four-hour window opening at 9:35 p.m. EST from Space Launch Complex 4E (SLC-4E) at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California; the backup is Thursday at 9:21 p.m. EST, SpaceX's mission page shows. The launch had first been set for Monday night, KSBY reported. No reason was given. The booster, flying for the seventh time after NROL-179, two Space Development Agency (SDA) missions and three Starlink missions, is to land on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You.

USSF-317 moves to Saturday night

USSF-317, set Tuesday for Friday at 11:09 p.m. EST, is now targeted for Saturday, Nov. 14, at 10:55 p.m. EST, with a backup on Sunday at 10:41 p.m. EST, according to SpaceX; no reason was given. Its booster makes its 11th flight. The FAA advisory still lists Friday night as the primary window and Saturday night as the backup.

III · Starlink

Starlink Mini 2 adds a swappable battery

The new portable dish runs up to 4.5 hours unplugged; Slate Aviation signs on for its fleet.

"Introducing Mini 2, the next-generation portable Starlink," @Starlink posted at 12:28 p.m. EST Tuesday. "Get up to 4.5 hours of connectivity on a single charge with the quick-swap battery, or plug in with USB-C. With built-in Wi-Fi, all you need is a clear view of the sky to get online in 60 seconds." Preorders are open by invitation in the US.

The dish is 23.1 millimeters thick, down from 38.5 millimeters for the original Mini, weighs about 3.5 pounds and keeps an IP67 dust- and waterproof rating, The Verge reported. Extra batteries will be sold separately, and it can also run from a 60-watt USB-C source. Invitations shared by users on X put the price at $999, including a year of Roam Unlimited service, the outlet said. "Mini2 is a gamechanger!" wrote Michael Nicolls, SpaceX's senior vice president of Starlink, and Elon Musk called it "Portable, lightning-fast Internet that works anywhere on Earth where Starlink is legal!" at 3:25 a.m. EST Wednesday.

Slate Aviation equips its fleet

"Slate Aviation is bringing @Starlink aboard its fleet, so owners and members can stream, take calls, and stay connected from takeoff to touchdown," the company posted Tuesday. Installations begin in December, with the full fleet to be equipped by mid-2027. @Starlink answered at 10:51 a.m. EST that it "will deliver fast, reliable internet onboard."

IV · Starlink Mobile

FCC opens its review of SpaceX's 800 MHz purchase

The Grain deal would give Starlink Mobile nationwide low-band airwaves; comments are due Dec. 1.

The FCC's Wireless Telecommunications Bureau said Tuesday that applications to transfer control of 546 paired 800 MHz licenses from Grain Management to Starlink Spectrum Holdings, a wholly owned SpaceX subsidiary, are acceptable for filing, in a public notice (DA 26-1218, WT Docket No. 26-313). The licenses hold up to 14 megahertz of paired spectrum across 3,224 counties in all 50 states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands, covering about 100% of the U.S. population. Comments and petitions to deny are due Dec. 1 and oppositions Dec. 8.

After the deal, SpaceX would hold up to 89 megahertz of spectrum in any area, including up to 14 megahertz below 1 GHz, the notice says. Conditions attached when Grain bought the licenses from T-Mobile in July, which require the spectrum to be put to rapid use for ground and direct-to-device service, would apply to SpaceX. Acceptance is not approval, Teslarati noted; it opens the public comment period.

Space use still pending

A separate SpaceX application, filed Oct. 27, to use the same 817–824 MHz and 862–869 MHz frequencies for supplemental coverage from space remains pending, the notice says. SpaceX said in October that the low-band spectrum would let Starlink Mobile's signal reach phones inside buildings and that most existing phones already support the band. Musk has called it "the last critical piece of the spectrum puzzle needed for SpaceX to provide complete phone coverage in America," Teslarati recalled.

V · AI & Compute

A solar-cell line for Starmind

SINGULUS will build heterojunction cell equipment for SpaceX's Bastrop satellite factory; a Grok Build flaw exposes shared apps.

"We have signed a major supply contract with SINGULUS to create a durable option for advanced solar cell manufacturing to support more than 100 GW of equipment as we accelerate the production of our Starmind satellites in Bastrop, Texas," SpaceX posted at 2:04 p.m. EST Tuesday. Musk added at 6:47 p.m. EST: "SpaceX has ordered 100 GW/year of HJT solar manufacturing equipment from Singulus in Germany. This is for Starmind to create a sentient sun."

The first turnkey line for heterojunction (HJT) solar cells has an order volume of more than €60 million and is to be delivered in the first half of 2027, SINGULUS said; it plans to deliver multiple additional lines under a strategic partnership begun in March 2026, when SpaceX provided significant funding, with more in October through a subordinated secured loan. Nicolls said the deal gives SpaceX a high-performance manufacturing path for Starmind's solar cells, pv magazine reported.

Grok Build links leave apps open

More than 35,000 apps made with Grok Build, SpaceXSI's AI app builder, were open to anyone who found their links, including 24 built by SpaceXSI itself, security firm Claroc found, Ars Technica reported. Each app gets a random name to the left of grok.me, which passive DNS services can list. SpaceXSI began removing its own 24 apps after Ars asked about them. A representative of Cursor, which SpaceXSI acquired, said the apps "are private by default and nothing goes public unless the creator chooses to share it."
