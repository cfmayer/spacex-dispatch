The Morning Picture

SpaceX slides 3.5% as its biggest AI customer debuts

Anthropic, which has committed up to $120.5 billion through 2029 to computing capacity from SpaceX's AI arm, opened its first trading day at $111.50 and briefly carried a fully diluted value above $2 trillion, more than SpaceX's, before a broad market slide pulled it back. SPCX closed down 3.53% at $138.43, The Motley Fool reported, as the S&P 500 fell 2.02%.

At Vandenberg, SpaceX scrubbed Thursday night's USSF-317 launch without giving a reason and will try again at 9:32 p.m. EST tonight. The CRS-35 cargo Dragon is set to leave the International Space Station (ISS) at 12:05 p.m. EST today. And SpaceX pushed back on reports of Starship debris on Mexican beaches, calling most social-media claims "outright fabrications or intentionally misleading."

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Dragon set to leave the station today

The CRS-35 cargo ship undocks at midday with research samples aboard; splashdown off California moves a few minutes earlier, to about 4:55 p.m. EST Saturday.

Pending weather, the Dragon will undock from the Harmony module's space-facing port at 12:05 p.m. EST on a command from SpaceX's control center in Hawthorne, California, with NASA coverage from 11:45 a.m., NASA's advisory says. An update on Thursday moved the targeted splashdown off the California coast to about 4:55 p.m. EST Saturday, Nov. 21, from 5:04 p.m.; NASA will not stream the splashdown and will post updates on its station blog. It is the capsule's third departure date: teams stood down from Wednesday and then, SpaceX said, from Thursday for weather at the splashdown sites.

The return load includes a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct used to study vascular aging and cardiovascular disease, frozen tumor organoids treated aboard the station, frozen samples of antibiotic-resistant bacteria and a lighting system that spent more than three years in orbit testing whether it helps astronauts sleep. Dragon is the only cargo craft flying today that can bring cargo back down; Northrop Grumman's Cygnus, Russia's Progress and Japan's HTV-X burn up on reentry, Space.com noted. The capsule arrived on Oct. 15 with more than 6,300 pounds of supplies.

On Thursday, NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney ran strength tests on the station's resistive exercise device, and Delaney, Anil Menon and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk serviced spacesuits in the Quest airlock, NASA's station blog said. The crew is also preparing for the Cygnus XL that launches on a Falcon 9 no earlier than Nov. 29 with about 11,000 pounds of cargo.

USSF-317 scrubbed, no reason given

SpaceX "scrubbed the launch of the USSF-317 mission tonight" and now targets a window opening at 9:32 p.m. EST Friday, Spaceflight Now posted at 10:21 p.m. EST Thursday; SpaceX did not state why, its report says. The classified Space Force mission is the sixth in a series of "R" launches; after each of the first five, 23 satellites were catalogued, though the Space Force has not confirmed details or deployments. Booster B1100 flies for the 11th time and is to land on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You, which would be the vessel's 235th landing and the 679th Falcon booster landing.

The target had already moved three times on Thursday, from 11:28 p.m. to 11:32 p.m. EST, then to 1:43 a.m. Friday, before the scrub, according to SpaceX mission-page changes logged by Launch Library 2. It would be SpaceX's first launch since Starship Flight 15 on Nov. 13.

Starship status

Flight 15 (Booster 22, Ship 42) launched from Starbase Pad 2 on Nov. 13, deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites and ended 22.5 hours later with the first catch of a ship; the booster's boostback burn "ended early due to low fuel mass, with the root cause under investigation" (SpaceX). The FAA's Nov. 13 license revision covered that single flight (Seeking Alpha). No date has been announced for Flight 16 or 17, and whether either flies from LC-39A in Florida is open (see Section IV). The FAA held its last virtual scoping meeting on more Starship launches from Starbase on Thursday; comments close Dec. 7 (Federal Register).

Two Vandenberg windows ten hours apart

With USSF-317 now set for Friday night, the FAA advisory shows SpaceX launch windows from Vandenberg on consecutive nights and mornings: USSF-317 opening at 9:32 p.m. Friday and NROL-136 opening at 7:30 a.m. Saturday, about ten hours later. Launch Library 2 lists both for SLC-4E. USSF-266 follows on Monday; after USSF-317, SpaceX has at least two more Space Force launches scheduled before the end of the month, Spaceflight Now says.

III · Starlink

Verizon's CEO: $300 billion to copy us

Dan Schulman says a Starlink phone carrier would take four to six years and end up as "the sixth entrant"; Lufthansa speeds up Starlink installs to ten jets a month.

If SpaceX CEO Elon Musk builds Starlink into a major U.S. phone carrier, he will have spent four to six years and about $300 billion on a copy of Verizon, Verizon CEO Dan Schulman told Fortune's Titans and Disruptors of Industry podcast. "At the end of the day, he might have a network that is pretty much exactly like our network today," Schulman said. "There's no secret sauce, there's no magic wand, there's no structural cost advantage that will come out. He'll have a network, and in four years, five, six years, he'll be the sixth entrant into the market," Fortune reported.

Schulman said the rooftop "micro cell towers" Musk described at the DealBook Summit on Wednesday would make SpaceX just another carrier, but he is not dismissing the threat: "I think the right thing to do is to believe everything that SpaceX says they're going to do, that they do, and they do it with excellence," he said. "The downside is when you're complacent about it." SpaceX's October announcement that Starlink would become "a major mobile carrier in the U.S." cut about $50 billion from the three big carriers' market values, and Verizon fell 8.7% that day, its worst since 2002, Fortune noted. JPMorgan analysts wrote last month that without a carrier partner SpaceX would need its own terrestrial network to compete.

Quick Hit

Design files in minutes. Starlink is connecting Cad Consults, an Accra, Ghana, firm that designs roads, bridges and buildings, so field crews at remote sites can send multi-gigabyte design files "in minutes instead of days," Starlink posted at 6:30 p.m. EST Thursday.

Lufthansa: ten more jets a month

Lufthansa Airlines plans to equip ten additional aircraft a month with Starlink and wants to earn revenue from the onboard internet, according to an internal letter from the airline's management board to staff, airliners.de reported on Friday. The letter, which listed the move among six first steps to answer the core brand's losses alongside 1,000 fewer full-time jobs at the Frankfurt and Munich hubs, did not say how access would be sold; the group had planned free access for status customers and Travel ID users. A spokesperson said the company had nothing to add.

The Lufthansa Group said in January it would fit its whole fleet of about 850 aircraft with Starlink by 2029, half of it by 2028, timing installations to maintenance downtime; the first aircraft of the core brand was equipped in early August, and 50 more across the group are due by year-end.

IV · Starship

SpaceX disputes Mexico's debris reports

After Flight 15's booster blew up off Starbase, Mexican authorities collected fragments on a Tamaulipas beach; SpaceX says few are clearly its own. The next ship and booster go into testing.

On Starship's 15th flight, "the Super Heavy booster correctly performed a safe abort when returning for a catch and ended its mission with a splashdown in the water off the coast of Starbase," SpaceX posted at 3:38 p.m. EST Thursday. When it received reports of possible debris in Mexican territory, the company said, it sent specialists to help identify it and lead any cleanup, and it is "on standby to send support if authorities in Mexico tell us it is actually needed." Partners on the ground "have found very little debris in their sweeps of the beach, and the small amount they have found are not clearly from SpaceX operations," SpaceX said, adding that "the majority of the social media posts online claiming widespread debris and damage are either outright fabrications or intentionally misleading" and that independent tests found the vehicles' materials pose no chemical, biological or toxicological risk.

The post quoted a Tuesday statement from Mexico's Federal Attorney for Environmental Protection (Profepa), which said its inspectors were at Playa Bagdad in Matamoros, Tamaulipas, identifying residue that began reaching Mexican waters that morning, that the protected-areas commission and the group Conibio Global had begun collecting it, and that the company would be asked to take responsibility. Conibio Global counted more than 1,500 pieces, and activists described them as large, mostly plastic fragments able to withstand very high temperatures, the Spanish news agency EFE reported.

SpaceX's post-flight analysis says one of the booster's 33 Raptor engines failed to reignite for the boostback burn, which ended early on low fuel; the booster's guidance, navigation and control system then triggered an automated abort to an offshore landing point in the Gulf, where it exploded moments before splashdown, Starlust reported. A day after the flight, SpaceX said its recovery teams were on site and its environmental team was recovering any debris washing ashore at Boca Chica Beach.

Flight 17 hardware heads to Massey's

"Starship Flight 17: Ship 44 rolling up Highway 4 to Massey's," NASASpaceflight posted at 2:52 p.m. EST Thursday; Booster 24 followed hours later for cryogenic proof testing, in which the vehicles are filled with super-cold liquid nitrogen to check their structure. Ship 43 and Booster 23, expected for Flight 16, were tested last month. Musk said at the TomorrowX Summit on Wednesday that Ship 42, the first ship caught, will likely go on public display rather than fly again "because what it has done is historic" (Starlust).

Florida debut in question

A newly released "high profile missions" slide in an air-traffic planning briefing no longer shows Starship's first launch from the Cape at all, the tracking account Space Notices posted at 11:51 a.m. EST Thursday. "I would not be surprised if SpaceX opts to fly a repeat of Flight 15 from Starbase (Flight 16), and then Flight 17 from 39A," NASASpaceflight wrote, noting that a Florida launch needs a booster and ship shipped on the barge You'll Thank Me Later and "a fair amount of 39A testing."

V · AI & Compute

Anthropic's debut, SpaceX's exposure

The largest IPO on record lifts Anthropic 17% on day one while SpaceX slips; its AI unit defends Grok in a London privacy lawsuit.

Anthropic's initial public offering (IPO), the largest on record, priced at $83 a share late Wednesday and opened at $111.50 shortly before noon on Thursday; after its market value briefly passed $2 trillion, momentum faded as all three major U.S. indexes fell more than 1%, Reuters reported. The stock closed at $97.17, up 17.07% from the offer price. "That money is coming from somewhere; investors are making changes in their portfolio to incorporate Anthropic," said Jed Ellerbroek, a portfolio manager at Argent Capital, who added that his expectations for data-center spending "have come down a little bit over the last month or two."

Anthropic is the biggest customer of SpaceX's AI unit: its IPO filing commits up to $120.5 billion through 2029 for computing capacity, with payments of about $3.1 billion a month once everything is delivered. "A contract that size is great for growth, but it ties SpaceX's results pretty closely to one customer, and any cut in Anthropic's spending would show up quickly," The Motley Fool's Johnny Rice wrote.

SpaceXSI: AI fakes "implausible," not private

SpaceXSI, the SpaceX unit formerly called xAI and then SpaceXAI, has filed its defense against a London High Court lawsuit by Jess Asato, a Labour member of Britain's Parliament, over sexualized images of her made with Grok, arguing that obviously fictitious AI-generated content cannot breach a right to privacy, Reuters reported. Its lawyers said it removed images that broke its terms, including a video depicting an assault, but that others would be seen by reasonable viewers as "AI-generated, fictitious and implausible," and that it "did not determine the content of the relevant users' prompts." "SpaceX SI wants to have it both ways," Asato said Thursday. She seeks an order forcing permanent technical measures so Grok cannot make manipulated images of her.

Quick Hit

Permit in hand. SpaceX won approval this week for its $24 billion data center and gas-fired power plant at Starbase as Texas resumed environmental permits for data centers, even as county commissioners denied its tax break, Investor's Business Daily reported.
