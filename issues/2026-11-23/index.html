The Morning Picture

Two launches from one pad in 32 hours

SpaceX launched USSF-317 for the Space Force from Vandenberg at 11:20 p.m. EST Friday and NROL-136 for the National Reconnaissance Office from the same pad at 7:23 a.m. EST Sunday, 32 hours, 3 minutes and 21 seconds apart, beating its previous turnaround record of 45 hours. In between, the CRS-35 cargo Dragon splashed down off Los Angeles on Saturday with research samples from the International Space Station (ISS).

SPCX fell 7.69% to $127.79 on Friday, below its $135 IPO price, after the Financial Times reported that lenders on SpaceX's $43 billion "Project Saturn" loan sought to bar the company from launching the AI chips that secure it into orbit; the S&P 500 dropped 3.70%. USSF-266 is next, now Tuesday at 10:55 a.m. EST, and NASASpaceflight reports Starship Flight 16 may fly from Texas rather than Florida.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

A 32-hour pad turnaround at Vandenberg

USSF-317 flew on its second night after a scrub, NROL-136 followed a day and eight hours later, and Dragon came home with research samples.

A Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4E (SLC-4E) at Vandenberg Space Force Base at 11:20 p.m. EST Friday on USSF-317, a classified Space Force mission, Space.com reported. SpaceX had passed up its Thursday-night opportunity, Spaceflight Now said. The booster, B1100, landed on the drone ship Of Course I Still Love You about 8.5 minutes after liftoff, its 11th launch and landing. SpaceX ended its webcast after the landing; it was the 121st Falcon 9 launch of 2026 and SpaceX's third Space Force mission this month.

At 7:23 a.m. EST Sunday the next Falcon 9 lifted off from the same pad with NROL-136, the 15th launch for the NRO's "proliferated architecture" of satellites built with SpaceX and Northrop Grumman and the agency's sixth launch of 2026, all on Falcon 9, according to Space.com. Booster B1097, on its 14th flight, returned to Landing Zone 4 (post). The NRO does not disclose how many satellites flew.

"Massive high five to the @SpaceX west coast team for a world record launch to launch turn from Pad 4e last night! 32 hr, 3 min, 21 sec! This smashed the previous record of 45 hrs from Pad 40. Team got there with several hours of margin. Next goal… the 24 hr turn!"

Kiko Dontchev, SpaceX vice president of launch, on X, as quoted by Space.com

SpaceX called it "our fastest turnaround between launches from the same pad." The gap beat the SLC-4E record by about 23 hours, NASASpaceflight said; Pad 40 is at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida. "32 hour launch pad recycle. Great work by SpaceX team," Elon Musk posted.

Dragon splashes down off Los Angeles

The CRS-35 cargo Dragon undocked from the station at 12:05 p.m. EST Friday and splashed down in the Pacific near Los Angeles at 5:03 p.m. EST Saturday, completing SpaceX's 35th resupply mission for NASA, the agency's station blog said. The return load included a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct for studying vascular aging, frozen tumor organoids, frozen samples of antibiotic-resistant bacteria and a lighting system tested for more than three years as a sleep aid for crews. "Splashdown of Dragon confirmed," SpaceX posted at 5:04 p.m. EST.

Starship status

With Booster 22 lost on Flight 15 before it could return for a catch, SpaceX may fly Flight 16 with Booster 23 and Ship 43 from Starbase rather than from Launch Complex 39A in Florida, aiming to prove the booster catch and repeat the ship catch first, NASASpaceflight reported. That would push a 39A flight to at least February. Booster 23 is slated for a static fire at the end of November once Pad 2 is ready, which could make it flight-ready just before Christmas; Ship 43 is slated for a static fire in mid-December and flight readiness in January. Booster 24 and Ship 44 would then likely take the first 39A flight. "Starship Flight 17: Booster 24 into full cryo testing," NASASpaceflight posted at 5:53 p.m. EST Sunday.

Ship 42 came back with chunks of ablative material scraped off by the tower's chopsticks; SpaceX has in mind a steel-wool mesh that can survive reentry and hold up to catches, NASASpaceflight said. Musk wrote on Saturday that "woven stainless steel matting on the ship side probably solves for zero refurbishment."

III · Starlink

Free service for flooded Brevard County

Starlink waives its fee through Dec 20 for homes hit by flooding on the Space Coast, and connects foster-care institutions in 16 countries.

"For new and existing residential customers affected by flooding in Brevard County, Starlink service is free through December 20," Starlink posted at 6:13 p.m. EST Friday. "This region is home to our neighbors on the Space Coast, and to Kennedy Space Center and Cape Canaveral, where SpaceX has launched astronauts, research, and spacecraft to orbit," the post said, adding that the company is in contact with local responders.

Starlink is also connecting foster homes across 16 countries in Africa and Latin America with the nonprofit BEB Global, which lets many institutions digitize records, track cases and help trace families, the company said. Musk shared a post about Starlink service at rural Honduran schools, including 200 students at Escuela Sergio Palacios in El Hatillo: "Starlink helps kids with education."

Most new customers see lower prices, Navi finds

About two-thirds of new Starlink locations in the U.S. are seeing significant discounts under the company's new capacity- and demand-based pricing, some of 30% to 50%, according to address-level data from Navi covering about 4,396 homes, Light Reading reported on Monday. In some areas the 100 Mbit/s plan sells for $27.50 a month, down from $55, while in a few places where Starlink is short of capacity the cheapest plan can cost up to $156 a month, up from about $130. Navi said on-demand surcharges in Alaska fell from $1,500 to $500. "What they're suddenly starting to do is aggressively price to demand and capacity," said Mike Tarr, Navi's general manager of data and insights.

Quick Hit

Starlink in Teslas. Asked about a built-in Starlink option for every Tesla model, Musk answered: "Hopefully available towards the end of next year." He added: "Except in China, as Starlink is illegal there."

Aer Lingus brings Starlink to its European fleet

Aer Lingus has begun rolling out free Starlink Wi-Fi on its short-haul fleet, starting with EI-DVM, an Airbus A320ceo in the airline's retro livery, and expects 29 short-haul aircraft to be equipped in early 2027, the airline said on Monday. The service already runs on its North American routes, where customers use an average of 1.8GB of data per connected device. "The response from our customers to Starlink Wi-Fi travelling to our North American destinations has been fantastic," said Susanne Carberry, Aer Lingus's chief customer officer.

IV · AI & Compute

Lenders want the collateral kept on the ground

Banks behind the $43 billion Project Saturn loan reportedly sought covenants against launching the Nvidia chips that secure it; Cursor tells Congress it may drop Chinese models.

Lenders behind SpaceX's $43 billion "Project Saturn" debt package sought covenants barring the company from launching the Nvidia AI chips pledged as collateral into orbit, where they would be nearly impossible to repossess in a default, the Financial Times reported on Friday, according to Investing.com. The report came a day after SpaceX completed a $32 billion bond sale, the largest tranche roughly $25 billion of investment-grade notes priced to yield above 8%.

Musk, without addressing the loan, wrote on Saturday that getting compute "turned on, cooled and working reliably is incredibly hard. The cash shortage is also going to be nuts next year. Even cumulative industry delays of a few months between paying for compute and getting it reliable enough to generate revenue will mean hundreds of billions of incremental dollars just for working capital. Where will that come from?"

Cursor may phase out Chinese models

Cursor, the AI coding company SpaceX bought in August, told House investigators it plans to "explore phasing out PRC-developed open-weight models because of geopolitical risk," according to a report by the House Homeland Security Committee and the House Select Committee on China, CNBC reported on Friday. Cursor's Composer 2 coding model was built on Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.5. "We aren't serving open source models," Musk replied to a post about the report.
