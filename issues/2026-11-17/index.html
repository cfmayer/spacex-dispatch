The Morning Picture

Wall Street cheers the ship catch as SPCX slips with tech

Analysts spent Monday praising Starship Flight 15. "With a ship catch, SpaceX has materially de-risked the longer-term TAMs across all 3 of its segments," Morgan Stanley wrote, and Raymond James called the flight "one of the most consequential technological achievements in modern aerospace history," CNBC and MarketWatch reported. The stock still fell 1.26% to $137.36 as the Nasdaq 100 dropped 2.67% in a broad technology selloff.

Elon Musk said the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) had turned down free Starlink terminals before Friday's nationwide ground stop, and that heat-shield material scraped off by the tower's arms will be replaced with "stainless steel wool." USSF-317 holds for Wednesday night at Vandenberg, and Antonio Gracias reported beneficial ownership of 5.5% of SpaceX's Class A shares, mostly through Valor entities.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Steel wool for the next chopsticks catch

Ship 42 is back on the ground at Starbase; Musk says the heat-shield material scraped off by the tower arms will be replaced, and the next ship will be much bigger.

"Starship 42 back on Earth," SpaceX posted at 4:49 p.m. EST Monday, after a day of releasing footage of Saturday's catch, including views from the tower and a camera riding on the ship during final approach. "View of Starship landing from the tower," Elon Musk wrote at 6:30 p.m., sharing the clip.

One detail drew attention: "You can really see the ablative getting scraped off by the chopsticks," the account @xdNiBoR noted. "That will be replaced with stainless steel wool for future flights," Musk replied at 9:05 p.m. When Marcus House (@MarcusHouse) asked whether that meant "some sort of material that survives entry and doesn't scrape off," Musk answered "Yes." He also agreed with Tim Dodd (@Erdayastronaut), who wrote that Starship "has about 18,000 tiles and only ONE fell off."

Starship V4 grows by 40%

"Starship V4 will be ~70m long, which is roughly a 40% length increase and allows >60% more propellant load," Musk wrote on Sunday evening. "Engine count will be raised to 9, with 3 more Raptor-Vacuum engines added, and these will be higher thrust V4 engines," Space.com reported. Today's V3 ship is 52 meters tall with six Raptor V3 engines; Musk said a fully stacked V4 is "probably closer to 150m height."

Quick Hit

Tom Mueller @lrocket "Was good to see @lrocket & @HansKoenigsmann at the first ship catch," Musk posted at 2:08 a.m. EST Tuesday, quoting a clip in which Mueller says Musk "really gets the best out of everybody, and that's why that company is so incredible."

What Wall Street saw

Citi analyst John Godyn, who attended the launch, raised his target to $280 and wrote that the catch is "one of the key milestones we've flagged along a multi-year path of upside toward $900+." Wells Fargo said the milestone "comes far ahead of schedule (vs our prior 1H:27 estimate)," and Bernstein said that "if the heat shield is found to be in good condition, we may expect to see this Starship reused on the next flight, Flight 16," CNBC reported. Deutsche Bank said its 2027–28 Starship cadence assumptions "should be meaningfully de-risked now."

Evercore ISI's Kutgun Maral called the mission a reminder that SpaceX is "at its core, a rocket company," and Mizuho's Brett Linzey called the weekend a "clear positive" for the shares, MarketWatch reported.

USSF-317 booster

The first stage flying USSF-317 previously launched NROL-105, USSF-385 and eight Starlink missions and will land on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You in the Pacific; a live webcast begins about 10 minutes before liftoff, SpaceX said. It would be SpaceX's first launch since Flight 15 on Nov. 13 (Launch Library 2).

Starship status

Flight 15 (Booster 22, Ship 42) launched from Starbase Pad 2 on Nov. 13 and ended with the first catch of a ship on Nov. 14; the booster was lost after its boostback burn "ended early due to low fuel mass, with the root cause under investigation" (SpaceX). The FAA's Nov. 13 license revision covered that single flight (Seeking Alpha). No Flight 16 date has been announced; it could be the first Starship launch from LC-39A in Florida (The New York Times). The FAA's virtual scoping meetings on more Starship launches from Starbase begin today at 2:00 p.m. EST and run through Thursday (Federal Register).

III · Starlink

Musk revives his FAA Starlink offer after the ground stop

Musk says the agency declined free terminals before Friday's outage; Ukraine is still waiting on Starshield, and Qatar Airways passes 31 million Starlink passengers.

"As I was saying … Free Starlinks for FAA were offered to Sec Duffy, but he said they were not needed. As it turns out, they were," Musk posted at 4:47 p.m. EST Monday, quoting his February 2025 post about the FAA's aging L3Harris network. The Department of Transportation has not responded publicly to his account, Teslarati reported.

The FAA says its legacy equipment became unstable at about 3:30 p.m. Friday when data began flooding its telecommunications network "at a rate of 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds," traced to a radar site in Amarillo, Texas, and a radio site in El Dorado, Arkansas, where L3Harris had deployed a new router. Instability hit 17 of 22 en-route centers, about 3,500 aircraft landed safely, and operators canceled about 12,462 flights that day, the agency said; it found no sign of a cyberattack.

Ukraine still waiting on Starshield

SpaceX has told Ukraine that the renewed limits on its Starshield military service are the result of a technical issue but gave no further explanation, Ukraine's chargé d'affaires in Washington, Denys Sienik, said, hromadske reported; Ukrainian officials say terminals lose service above 161 kilometers per hour over occupied territory. "Our interceptor drones lose connection with their operators at a speed of 161 kilometers per hour and simply cannot catch up with Russian jet-powered 'shaheds,'" President Volodymyr Zelenskyy wrote, Euronews reported.

Democratic Senator Jeanne Shaheen and a fellow Democratic senator asked Musk to preserve all records on the restrictions since Sept. 1, including correspondence with the White House and the Pentagon, ahead of planned investigations, Meduza reported, citing The Wall Street Journal. Musk has said Starshield is owned and controlled by the U.S. government.

Qatar Airways passes 31 million passengers

Qatar Airways said its free Starlink Wi-Fi has served more than 31 million passengers since October 2024 across more than 117,000 flights, with up to 373 connected flights a day and 170 widebody aircraft equipped, more than 95% of its planned widebody deployment, The Peninsula reported.

Quick Hit

North Star The UK offshore operator's head of IT, Bruce Catto, said Starlink "has changed everything for us" across 36 vessels that once relied on links of about 1 megabit per second; Musk shared the interview Monday (TeslaNorth).

IV · The Competition

FAA orders a Vulcan mishap investigation

United Launch Alliance will lead the inquiry into Sunday's loss of 40 Amazon Leo satellites; an analyst points to the first stage.

"The FAA is requiring ULA to conduct a mishap investigation," the agency said in a statement posted by Spaceflight Now at 12:27 p.m. EST Monday; it will oversee the United Launch Alliance (ULA)-led investigation and approve its final report, and it activated two debris response areas during the failure. Preliminary reports point to an issue with Vulcan's first stage, "raising the possibility of a problem" with its Blue Origin-built engines, KeyBanc's Michael Leshock wrote, MarketWatch reported.

"With Vulcan's recent failed launch to reach orbit, we view SpaceX's continued execution and deployment of its V3 satellites as incrementally positive," RBC analyst Ken Herbert wrote Monday, according to the same report.

V · AI & Compute

Musk: SpaceX plans on zero OpenAI sales

Musk answers Sam Altman's claim that chipmakers double-count OpenAI demand, and Anthropic, reported to be SpaceX's biggest compute customer, prices its IPO Wednesday.

The account @firesidealpha posted a clip of OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman saying chipmakers are "double counting" OpenAI demand, each assuming OpenAI will buy all of their chips. "SpaceX is assuming zero sales to OpenAI. If OpenAI is not seeing demand rising super fast, then something is wrong with their products," Musk replied at 8:08 a.m. EST Tuesday.

Anthropic's SpaceXAI contracts

Anthropic plans to price its initial public offering (IPO) on Wednesday and begin trading on Nasdaq as ANT on Thursday; its amended S-1 describes up to $120.5 billion of SpaceXAI graphics processing unit (GPU) capacity that either side can cancel on 90 days' notice once initial periods end in January and March 2027, TechTimes reported. Anthropic is the customer SpaceX calls "Customer B," which accounted for 34.5% of SpaceX's third-quarter revenue, according to a Bloomberg report cited by TechTimes.
