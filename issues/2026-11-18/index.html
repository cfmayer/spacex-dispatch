The Morning Picture

SpaceX's GPU lease heads to bond buyers

Debt backing SpaceX's roughly $40 billion lease of Nvidia Vera Rubin chips could start selling to investors as soon as this week through a special-purpose vehicle that keeps it off SpaceX's balance sheet, Bloomberg and Semafor reported. The stock rose 3.16% to $141.70 on Tuesday while the Nasdaq 100 fell 0.72%, and Nvidia's quarterly holdings report showed 130.5 million SpaceX Class A shares.

Chief financial officer Bret Johnsen told an Austin investor conference that SpaceX builds data centers for "low-to-mid $30s" per watt and plans its first orbital-compute satellites in 2027. Two flights slipped a day: USSF-317 now targets Thursday night at Vandenberg, and weather at the California splashdown zones pushed the CRS-35 Dragon's departure from the space station to Thursday.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Splashdown weather holds Dragon at the station

The CRS-35 cargo ship stays docked another day; SpaceX releases the tower's view of the first ship catch.

NASA and SpaceX stood down from Wednesday's planned 11:05 a.m. EST undocking of the cargo Dragon "due to unfavorable weather conditions at the splashdown sites off the coast of California," NASA said at 1:44 a.m. EST. The next undocking opportunity is 12:05 p.m. EST Thursday, Nov. 19, after a weather review at noon Wednesday. "Teams will continue to keep an eye on weather," SpaceX posted at 1:28 a.m.

The spacecraft has been docked for 34 days after arriving on Oct. 15 with more than 6,300 pounds of supplies for SpaceX's 35th commercial resupply services (CRS) mission. It is bringing home a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct used to study vascular aging, frozen tumor organoids, frozen samples of antibiotic-resistant bacteria and a lighting system tested for more than three years as a sleep aid for astronauts, NASA said. The capsule will undock from the Harmony module's space-facing port on a command from SpaceX's facility in Hawthorne, California; it launched Oct. 13 on a Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. NASA will not stream the splashdown.

The catch, from the tower

"The Starbase tower chopsticks catching their first Starship. This unique architecture was designed for full and rapid reusability, enabling repeat launches and landings from the same pad," SpaceX posted at 5:09 p.m. EST Tuesday with new footage of Saturday's catch. Spaceflight Now published its start-to-finish report on the mission that evening, calling the catch "a crucial step" toward a fully and rapidly reusable vehicle. The catch came three years to the week after Flight 2, when Ship 25 became "the first Starship to reach space" before its flight termination system fired about eight minutes in, NASASpaceflight recalled on Wednesday.

Quick Hit

Gavin Baker The Atreides Management investor urged parents to take children to a Starship launch in a clip Musk quoted: "I'd say probably half of adults cry at a launch." "True, Starship launches are incredibly exciting," Musk replied at 8:55 p.m. EST Tuesday.

USSF-317 moves a day

SpaceX is now "targeting Thursday, November 19" for the Space Force mission, with a four-hour window opening at 9:46 p.m. EST and a backup at 11:26 p.m. Friday, its mission page says; on Tuesday it listed 10:00 p.m. Wednesday. No reason was given. Launch Library 2 logged the change at 11:09 a.m. EST Tuesday. It is the fifth move since Nov. 9: the target has stepped a day or more at a time from 11:23 p.m. EST Thursday, Nov. 12, through Nov. 13, 14, 17 and 18, according to RocketRune's change log. The FAA advisory lists a backup window from 9:32 p.m. Friday to 2:15 a.m. Saturday. The booster, on its 11th flight, previously launched NROL-105, USSF-385 and eight Starlink missions; residents of Santa Barbara, San Luis Obispo and Ventura counties may hear sonic booms. It would be SpaceX's first launch since Flight 15 on Nov. 13 (Launch Library 2).

Starship status

Flight 15 (Booster 22, Ship 42) launched from Starbase Pad 2 on Nov. 13, deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites and ended 22.5 hours later with the first catch of a ship on Nov. 14; the booster's boostback burn "ended early due to low fuel mass, with the root cause under investigation," and it was diverted offshore (SpaceX). The FAA's Nov. 13 license revision covered that single flight (Seeking Alpha). No Flight 16 date has been announced; it could be the first Starship launch from LC-39A in Florida (The New York Times). The FAA's virtual scoping meetings on more Starship launches from Starbase run through Thursday (Federal Register).

III · Starlink

Duffy explains passing on free Starlinks

The Transportation Secretary explains why the agency passed on free terminals, and SpaceX's finance chief sets a 2028 target for 5G service to ordinary phones.

"We thought that the best method is to have a network that's based on the ground," Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Tuesday when asked about Musk's account that the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) had turned down free Starlink terminals before Friday's nationwide ground stop. Duffy called Starlink's speed "remarkable," said the FAA uses it for some assets in Alaska and said an earlier version had technical issues in testing at the agency's technology laboratory, Benzinga reported. He said the routers being replaced were about 25 years old and should have been replaced in 2006.

Musk answered the clip at 4:55 p.m. EST with a facepalm emoji. "I wrote this 18 months ago," he added at 12:26 a.m. Wednesday, quoting his February 2025 post that "the Verizon communication system to air traffic control is breaking down very rapidly" and that Starlink terminals were being sent "at NO COST to the taxpayer on an emergency basis."

Direct-to-cell: 5G to phones in 2028

Starlink broadband now serves more than 13 million people, chief financial officer Bret Johnsen told the TomorrowX Summit in Austin on Tuesday. SpaceX plans to start launching next-generation direct-to-cell satellites in 2027 and is targeting "true 5G-quality service" to unmodified phones in the first half of 2028; the first constellation of about 600 satellites was a proof of concept for texting and emergency messages, TMT Breakout reported. He said satellites can backhaul the traffic, allowing smaller phased-array ground installations instead of large towers. SpaceX shared the talk at 4:20 p.m.

IV · Starbase

Texas grants the air permit for a Starbase gas plant

State regulators clear 750 megawatts of turbines for a supercomputing campus as SpaceX sends Brownsville another $30 million for water.

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) issued an air permit on Monday for a 750-megawatt plant of 35 natural-gas turbines on a 444-acre site near Brownsville, annexed to the city of Starbase in September, to power a SpaceX supercomputing data center; the plant will not connect to the state grid, Politico reported. The permit allows up to 248 tons of nitrogen oxides a year, just under the 250-ton level that triggers a fuller review with a public hearing.

The agency resumed data-center permit reviews after Gov. Greg Abbott's office told it on Nov. 13 that grid officials had finished gathering information on community impacts, MLQ.ai reported, citing The Texas Tribune. FracTracker Alliance and Public Citizen had filed a complaint three days earlier alleging construction began without a permit. Starbase has posted notice of a Dec. 9 hearing on a SpaceX property-tax abatement request for the project; SpaceX has said it plans to invest more than $24 billion in an AI supercomputing campus at Starbase.

$70 million toward Brownsville water

SpaceX "provided another $30 million to the Brownsville Public Utilities Board," bringing its contributions to $70 million of the $220 million it has committed to secure drinking water supplies, the company posted at 10:46 a.m. EST Tuesday.

V · AI & Compute

Inside the $40 billion GPU lease

Bonds tied to SpaceX's Vera Rubin chips draw an A- preliminary rating, the finance chief lays out the compute math, and Nvidia shows a bigger stake.

A special-purpose vehicle managed by Apollo-backed Atlas SP will buy the Nvidia chips and issue asset-backed securities, with Goldman Sachs and Apollo underwriting a sale that could begin as soon as this week, Bloomberg reported on Tuesday; borrowing this way lets SpaceX keep its investment-grade ratings. Apollo will lend close to $20 billion upfront and sell it on later; the bulk of the seven-part deal, $35.5 billion, will price at 8.3% and 7.75%, and riskier slices under $3 billion could exceed 9%, Semafor reported at 7:53 a.m. EST Wednesday.

Rating agency KBRA assigned preliminary A- ratings to about $27.2 billion of senior notes and $2.4 billion of subordinated notes from Saturn Funding Trust, which will finance Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 equipment leased to SpaceX for about five years on "hell-or-high-water" terms with quarterly payments that fully pay down the debt, KBRA said. SpaceX announced the commitment on Oct. 26, with GPU deliveries expected in the first half of 2027 (SpaceX).

Johnsen: under a year to pay back

Johnsen said SpaceX's first couple of gigawatts of ground compute cost "low-to-mid $30s" per watt, against roughly $50–60 for the industry, and that its hosting deals pay back in under a year. SpaceX is building its own investment-casting plant for turbine blades after a supplier quoted 18–24 months, and keeps capacity on shorter and spot contracts rather than long commitments, TMT Breakout reported. On orbit, he set first compute satellites for 2027, a constellation ramp in 2028 and gigawatt-scale capacity in 2029, built on the Starlink V3 platform with graphics processing units (GPUs), more solar power and deployable radiators.

Nvidia's stake and Anthropic's pricing day

Nvidia's quarterly holdings report, filed Monday, lists 130,472,925 SpaceX Class A shares worth $19.68 billion as of Sept. 30 (13F-HR), a raised stake according to Investing.com. Anthropic, reported to be SpaceX's biggest compute customer, sets its initial public offering (IPO) price on Wednesday for 825 million shares at an indicated $70 to $78 and is expected to trade on Nasdaq as ANT on Thursday, Euronews reported. Johnsen said Anthropic's projected growth needs far more capacity than any compute it brings in-house.
