The Morning Picture

A 7.8% jump, and a lock-up opens

SPCX closed up 7.79% at $159.31 on Wednesday, on 219 million shares, more than twice its 30-session average, after SpaceX "posted its first profit as a public company," Reuters reported. Analysts lifted price targets across the board; JPMorgan's Doug Anmuth more than doubled his to $500, writing that "SpaceX's ambitions are bigger than any company's we've seen," CNBC reported.

From Thursday, up to 1.3 billion locked-up Class A shares, 28% of those under the 180-day lock-up, can be sold, followed by up to 328.4 million on Saturday and the rest on December 8, under the prospectus. A Starlink launch from California was scrubbed for weather Wednesday evening and tries again tonight.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Thunderstorms halt Starlink 15-15 a second time

The 36th flight of booster B1063 is now set for Thursday evening from Vandenberg.

SpaceX moved Wednesday's target to 7:22 p.m. EDT, warning that "the weather, however, may be a complicating factor," and then to 7:38:57 p.m., Spaceflight Now posted. At 7:04 p.m. it called the attempt off: "Poor weather persists at Vandenberg. With thunderstorms approaching the launchpad, SpaceX is scrubbing the launch of Starlink 15-15 for today. The next launch opportunity is Thursday, Oct. 22, weather and range permitting," Spaceflight Now wrote. Tuesday's first try ended with an apparent technical issue.

The Falcon 9 carries 27 Starlink V2 Mini satellites. Its first stage, B1063, is flying for a 36th time and will try to land on the drone ship Of Course I Still Love You in the Pacific; Thursday's window opens at 5:45 p.m. EDT, according to Spaceflight Now, and Launch Library 2 lists a 6:40 p.m. target with a "Go" status.

Starship Flight 15

Pad 2's booster quick disconnects (BQDs) were "purging. Hopefully ahead of Booster 22 arriving soon," NASASpaceflight posted at 2:00 p.m. EDT Wednesday, a day after a full test of the pad's water deluge system. Flight 15 is targeting November 8, according to NASASpaceflight, with Ship 42 and Booster 22.

"For Flight 15, which we expect to happen next month, we're going to attempt to catch both the booster and the ship," Musk said on Tuesday's call; President Gwynne Shotwell said SpaceX expects the license to "include return to launch site and tower catch for both stages" (transcript). Neither the FAA nor SpaceX has announced a launch date.

III · Starlink

United: "We already see customers moving"

Half of United's flights will have Starlink by the end of this week, its chief executive said.

"We're going to cross the 50% threshold this week with 50% of flights on Starlink," United Airlines Chief Executive Scott Kirby said in a television interview on Wednesday. "By the end of next year, we'll finish Starlink. We already see customers moving from other airlines today." He called the evidence "mostly anecdotal today," and said United's recent status-match offer drew about four times the usual applications, TeslaNorth reported. "Starlink moves the needle," Musk posted at 12:54 p.m. EDT over a clip of the interview.

United says Starlink is on more than 640 aircraft, with about three more added each day, and expects 61% availability by the end of 2026 and 87% by June 1, 2027 (TeslaNorth). SpaceX said about 3,800 aircraft had Starlink at the end of September.

Quick Hit

Telecom stocks slid. T-Mobile US fell 2.5% and Verizon 2.2% on Wednesday as SpaceX's results and its recent spectrum purchase "fanned fears that it could become a formidable competitor in telecoms," Reuters reported; AT&T sank 6.5% despite beating estimates.

IV · The Street

Targets rise, JPMorgan's to $500

Ten firms raised price targets after the results; HSBC kept a Hold.

"SPCX is proving the durability of its AI revenue growth and its ability to meet ambitious deployment timelines, moving investor forecasts higher as doubts begin to dissipate," Barclays analyst Anthony Valentini wrote, raising his target to $300 from $254. Citi, going to $240 from $200, said that if SpaceX hits key milestones "in the upcoming, catalyst-rich ~2-3 years without faltering, then we see a path to generating over ~$1 trillion in revenue," CNBC reported.

Morgan Stanley kept its $300 target, putting SPCX at 15.7 times its new 2028 earnings estimate of $9.45 a share at a $160 price. HSBC, with a Hold and a $150 target, noted "the lack of historical data and guidance" (CNBC). Bank of America later raised its target to $275 from $235 and Pivotal Research to $260 from $230, Yahoo Finance shows.

V · AI & Compute

"A shot at exceeding 10GW next year"

Musk answered a claim that SpaceX will build a quarter of new U.S. compute in 2027.

Replying to a post quoting investor Brad Gerstner as saying SpaceX "is building out 25% of all new compute in America next year," Musk wrote at 9:42 p.m. EDT: "We have a shot at exceeding 10GW next year." To a post suggesting his remark that SpaceX "could rent out our compute several times over" was bullish for smaller cloud providers, he replied: "Only if they can get their compute online and reliable, which is extremely difficult to do at scale." Demand for compute "appears substantially ahead of supply," Goldman Sachs wrote (CNBC).

Quick Hit

Southaven. Hundreds of electricians lined up at a Memphis union hall for data-center jobs, some citing offers above $8,000 a week. "Elon Musk is paying the most money in the country right now with this SpaceX little job that they got down there in Southaven," one told Action News 5. "Cool," Musk wrote.
