The Morning Picture

SpaceX tells the FAA it wants 10,000 launches a year

SpaceX told federal aviation regulators that it anticipates conducting 10,000 launches a year itself, and that the Federal Aviation Administration's air traffic control structure and processes, not rockets, are what stand in the way, TeslaNorth reported. The comments answer a Department of Transportation and FAA request for information on where to site new spaceports and how to give critical launch corridors priority in U.S. airspace, filed in docket FAA-2026-9736, whose comment window closed on October 26. The FAA's own August 25 notice forecast about 10,000 licensed launches and reentries a year by 2035; SpaceX put today's level at 182 licensed launches and 7 licensed reentries in 2025 (TeslaNorth).

"10k Starship launches/year is just from Starbase Louisiana," Elon Musk posted at 2:07 a.m. EDT Wednesday, quoting a summary of the filing. According to the filing, the planned Louisiana spaceport is designed for about 30 launches and reentries per day (Benzinga, Stocktwits). To a user who wrote that SpaceX "rewrites aviation control software to launch more," Musk replied at 3:58 a.m.: "We address the limiting factor."

SpaceX pointed to Cleared, its real-time launch-status and airspace platform, which it said most major U.S. airlines already use at no cost along with its API, and asked that such data be built into FAA flow-management and controller tools so hazard areas can shrink and move with the vehicle instead of closing large blocks of sky for long windows. None of the filings approves any launches: licensing, environmental review and air-traffic integration still have to follow (TeslaNorth).

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Ship 42 fires all six engines

A full-duration static fire cleared the way for Flight 15, while Ship 43 and Booster 23 move toward Flight 16.

"Starship Flight 15: Ship 42 conducts a six-engine static fire test," NASASpaceflight posted at 5:46 p.m. EDT Tuesday, adding a minute later: "Full duration" (post). The test followed the ship's single-engine static fire on Monday. "Ship 42 has completed both its single- and six-engine static fire tests, paving the way for Flight 15 in November," the outlet posted at 7:59 p.m.

The ship ran its six engines for roughly 60 seconds on the static fire stand at SpaceX's Massey Outpost, according to Next Spaceflight, whose test log shows the earlier single-engine firing demonstrated a deorbit burn. Ship 42 completed three rounds of cryogenic proof testing, on September 3, September 4 and October 9, before moving to engine tests (Next Spaceflight).

Two flows in parallel

"Starship Flight 16: Ship 43 has returned to the production site from its Pad 2 chopstick tests, with Booster 23 set to head out to Massey's for cryotesting. This duo is expected to sail to KSC to be the first Starship flight from 39A," NASASpaceflight posted at 12:24 a.m. EDT Wednesday. At 12:59 a.m. it added: "Here comes Booster 23. SpaceX is running two flows in parallel for Flights 15 and 16."

III · Starlink

United's chief calls Starlink its "biggest competitive advantage"

The airline is fitting about three jets a day and says it will have Starlink on its whole fleet by the end of 2027.

United Airlines is adding Starlink to about three aircraft a day and already has it on 661 jets, CEO Scott Kirby said on the Cheeky Pint podcast with John Collison and Ben Thompson released Tuesday, TeslaNorth reported. Starlink is on 48% of United flights and will reach 50% next week, after more than 5.4 million passengers used it in September. The airline had planned to stop at about 65% of the fleet before passenger feedback changed the plan; full-fleet coverage is now due by the end of 2027 (TeslaNorth).

"I think it's the biggest competitive advantage. And it's temporary," Kirby said, Teslarati reported. "Everybody eventually is going to have high speed low earth orbit internet connectivity. It's going to be a must-have. But we are going to have a few years of being the only airline that has it at scale, and that's our mission." Los Angeles is set to become United's first hub with Starlink on every flight in the first half of 2027, and the United app will show the odds that a booked flight has Starlink (TeslaNorth).

FCC votes Thursday on 482 MHz for satellite-to-phone

At its open meeting at 10:30 a.m. EDT Thursday, the Federal Communications Commission will consider a proposal that would make an additional 482 megahertz of spectrum available for Supplemental Coverage from Space, eliminate what it calls burdensome requirements, and consider harmonizing those rules with the Mobile Satellite Service rules, according to the FCC agenda. The meeting also takes up a proposal to auction 25 megahertz in the 1675–1695 MHz and 2020–2025 MHz bands for terrestrial, direct-to-device or hybrid use.

The proposal would expand the eligible bands from five to 13, Capacity reported. AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile, with the trade group CTIA, have asked the FCC to drop the 2.5 GHz and 3.45 GHz bands, arguing that satellite signals would degrade 5G networks; T-Mobile said a cell site could lose up to 96% of its uplink coverage area. A vote would adopt a notice of proposed rulemaking and open a comment period, not final rules (Capacity).

Report: ad bidder targeted Starlink users

Researchers at TrendAI, a unit of Trend Micro, concluded that Israeli ad-tech company Discover-Tech was one of the biggest sources of digital ads shown to Starlink users and the No. 1 bidder on Starlink traffic in more than 100 countries, likely to geolocate or otherwise surveil them, Reuters reported Tuesday, citing six people familiar with the matter. TrendAI's October 15 report called the bidder "Shadow-Earth-79" without naming it. Citizen Lab and Confiant named Discover-Tech the same day, and Google, Comcast and other companies said they had removed it from their ad platforms. SpaceX and Discover-Tech did not respond to requests for comment (Reuters).

TrendAI's report said the bidder was the top buyer of ads in Syria, Libya, Yemen and South Sudan, where "the demand for legitimate Western advertising in sanctioned or active-conflict states is effectively zero," and won "an implausibly dominant share" of bids in countries including Hungary, Israel, Egypt, Azerbaijan and Venezuela. It assessed the activity as "targeting and tracking infrastructure rather than a commercial advertising operation" (Reuters).

IV · AI & Compute

Anthropic puts SpaceX compute at up to $120.5 billion

The AI lab's IPO filing caps the deal; separately, Musk promised a quick fix to Grok Bot's payment flow.

Anthropic could pay SpaceX up to $120.5 billion for compute through 2029, the AI lab disclosed in its S-1 filing on Monday, Yahoo Finance reported. Monthly payments would rise to about $3.1 billion once all contracted capacity is delivered, which Anthropic expects in December 2026. The figure is a maximum: the agreements have initial periods ending in January and March 2027, after which either side can terminate on 90 days' notice. At $3.1 billion a month, Anthropic alone would bring SpaceX about $9.3 billion a quarter, against $14.575 billion of consolidated third-quarter revenue (Yahoo Finance).

"Major fail, will get fixed pronto"

"Key product feedback for team @bot and @elonmusk. I hit the paywall on grokbot. There are so many steps to pay that ive failed 2x. Are you tracking failed pay attempts?" Zynga founder Mark Pincus posted at 1:32 p.m. EDT Tuesday, adding that the product should "give some limited free use daily." Musk replied at 2:19 p.m.: "Major fail, will get fixed pronto. Thanks for the feedback."

Earlier, Musk shared a video of new Grok Bot features posted by @mattyp, whose chapter list includes Pages, Vault, 1Password and Apple integrations, video creation, new bot characters and X Spaces. Overnight he quote-posted users' praise, including a clip in which investor Gavin Baker calls Grok Bot "the most impactful AI product to me and to the firm that we've had yet" ("Grok @Bot rocks") and analyst Pierre Ferragu's post that his Grok Bot "is promoted to fully-fledged team member" ("Hire Grok @Bot, you won't regret it").
