The Morning Picture

Starlink Mobile goes live in Brazil

"Starlink Mobile is now live in Brazil with @vivobr! 50M customers can use apps and messaging in cellular dead zones — with 7M phones already connected. This is the first nationwide service to cover the entire country," Starlink posted at 11:53 a.m. EDT Monday. Michael Nicolls shared the announcement: "Starlink Mobile in Brazil with @vivobr !"

Vivo calls the service Vivo Satélite. Customers on eligible plans can use messaging apps, maps and the carrier's support channel where there is no tower signal, without the data counting against their monthly allowance, and the service is free for a limited time, O Globo reported. Video calls and general web browsing stay blocked. No sign-up or app is needed: the phone must support LTE or 5G, run the latest software and have data roaming switched on, and it needs a clear view of the sky.

The link runs in the 2.5 GHz band at up to 100 watts of transmit power. Brazil's telecom regulator, Anatel, set up a supervised test regime in June, and the authorization has been renewed to April 22, 2029, with commercial use allowed under the experimental rules (O Globo).

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Starlink 15-16 flies; Ship 42 fires

Falcon 9 lifted off from Vandenberg early Tuesday, and Starbase ran a ship static fire and a catch rehearsal on Monday night.

Falcon 9 lifted off from Vandenberg Space Force Base at 3:25 a.m. EDT Tuesday with the Starlink 15-16 mission, "A post-midnight takeoff," Spaceflight Now posted. The booster, B1082, was flying for the 26th time, and it landed after the launch (NASASpaceflight, touchdown). At 4:29 a.m. EDT SpaceX posted: "Deployment of 27 @Starlink satellites confirmed."

Ship 42 and Ship 43

At Starbase, Texas, the ship for Flight 15 ran an engine test on Monday night. "Ship 42 is setting up for a single-engine static fire shortly. Ship 43 is practising what it's like to be caught," NASASpaceflight posted at 9:58 p.m. EDT, and at 10:18 p.m.: "Starship Flight 15: Ship 42 conducts a single-engine static fire test at SpaceX Massey's" (post).

Ship 43, assigned to Flight 16, had rolled to Pad 2 earlier on Monday, "likely for catch testing ahead of Flight 15," wvmattz posted. By 2:02 a.m. EDT Tuesday it was "back on the ground after catch-related testing similar to what Ship 42 did," efraser77 posted.

Quick Hit

Raptor thrust. "Raptor 4 will be 300 tons of thrust at sea level and hopefully about the same mass," Elon Musk posted at 9:58 a.m. EDT Monday. Later he added: "We can only reliably get to around 230 t-F with Raptor 3."

III · Starlink

Verizon's chief: "no shortcut"

Verizon's CEO put a four-to-six-year, $300 billion to $500 billion price on a Starlink Mobile network in the U.S.; Musk answered "2 or 3 years."

"There is no magic wand. There's no secret sauce. There's no shortcut," Verizon CEO Daniel Schulman told analysts on the company's third-quarter earnings call on Monday, Yahoo Finance reported. "Their service just can't compete with a terrestrial network in suburban and urban areas, where like 98% of our revenues are," he said. "We're somewhere between 100 to 1000 times more efficient than they are."

Schulman estimated an all-in cost of $300 billion to $500 billion over the next six to seven years, assuming a buildout of at least four to six years. Replying to a post by Sawyer Merritt that summarized the remarks, Musk wrote at 1:40 p.m. EDT: "Will take 2 or 3 years." He did not comment on the cost figures.

SpaceX is in talks with telecom infrastructure companies, reportedly including Crown Castle, for access to at least 80,000 U.S. cell sites, Bloomberg reported on Thursday. Earlier this month SpaceX agreed to buy up to 14 megahertz of paired low-band spectrum in the 800 MHz range, a deal reported at about $8 billion that still needs Federal Communications Commission (FCC) approval. Of a SpaceX network built to that scale, Schulman said: "Frankly, at that point, I welcome the opportunity to compete against them."

Carriers lobby the FCC

Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile, with the wireless trade association CTIA, are lobbying the FCC to keep current cell-service spectrum rules and oppose changes that would make it easier for satellite companies such as SpaceX to offer cellular service, Yahoo Finance reported. "The Commission's buildout decisions have real consequences for consumers who rely on ever-increasing wireless capacity to access the modern economy and support their day-to-day living," CTIA wrote in a letter filed Thursday.

Their proposals include blocking satellite providers from 5G frequencies used for wireless service and letting wireless providers keep control of licensed spectrum. At Wednesday's AT&T earnings call, Chief Executive John Stankey said of a new network: "It's not something that gets done overnight." The FCC's open meeting on Thursday includes votes on more spectrum for Supplemental Coverage from Space (Broadband Breakfast).

IV · AI & Compute

"Probability of 20GW is currently rising"

Musk recast his compute forecast, and agreed with Michael Dell's description of him as a customer.

Replying to Peter Diamandis, who had written that Musk "says he could bring 20 gigawatts of compute online next year," Musk posted at 12:27 p.m. EDT Monday: "I said 20GWs next year is extremely unlikely, just not 0%, whereas the probability was 0% even a few months ago. Probability of 20GW is currently rising with time."

A clip from an interview with Michael Dell, shared by firesidealpha, has Dell calling Musk the "most demanding customer there is," and the interviewer noting that "Dell did half of the servers in the Colossus One AI supercomputer for SpaceX." Musk replied at 12:18 p.m. EDT: "True."
