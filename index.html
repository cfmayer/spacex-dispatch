The Morning Picture

Another SpaceX share for every child, every year, and USSF-380 confirmed for Saturday night

Elon Musk said on Thanksgiving that he will donate "another share of SpaceX to all kids in America every year," extending the one-time gift of two shares per active Trump Account that SpaceX and Musk announced on Wednesday. SPCX rose more than 7% in overnight trading and was 4% higher before Friday's open, CNBC reported. New details of Wednesday's Starmind license show a first satellite about 70 meters across and six orbital shells, and the FCC turned down objections from Amazon Leo, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society, NASASpaceFlight reported.

SpaceX has confirmed a Falcon 9 launch of the USSF-380 mission for the U.S. Space Force from California at 11:39 p.m. EST Saturday, and a Cygnus XL cargo ship is set to follow from Florida at 10:47 a.m. EST Sunday. Starlink is offering half-price home service for six months through Cyber Monday. US stock markets close early today, at 1:00 p.m. EST, according to the NYSE.

I · Shares for the Next Generation

"Another share of SpaceX to all kids in America every year"

A one-time gift becomes a yearly one; SPCX climbs outside regular trading hours.

Replying at 4:24 p.m. EST Thursday to a post that challenged readers to "name a more awesome act of philanthropy" than the Trump Account pledge, Musk wrote: "I will be donating another share of SpaceX to all kids in America every year." At 7:26 p.m. EST he added "Vital that children have a stake in the future," quoting a post that put the number of American children at about 70 million. Musk did not say how the yearly shares would be distributed or whether they would go through Trump Accounts, Teslarati noted.

The yearly pledge builds on Wednesday's announcement that SpaceX will contribute one newly issued Class A share to every currently active Trump Account, with Musk personally matching the company's gift, Bloomberg reported. Musk has said the company's share is to be transferred next month and his own when his lockup expires in June.

What it adds up to

At Wednesday's closing price of $127.44, one share for 70 million children works out to roughly $9 billion a year, a figure that would rise or fall with the stock, Teslarati calculated. Combined with President Gwynne Shotwell's pledge of more than 2 million shares, the first round already promised totals about 142 million shares worth roughly $18.1 billion, the site said.

The stock overnight

SpaceX shares jumped more than 7% on Blue Ocean ATS, an alternative trading system that resumed at 8:00 p.m. EST on Thanksgiving, and were last 4% higher in premarket trading amid a broader tech rebound, CNBC reported. Nearly 70 million Trump Accounts had been created as of October, according to the White House, and families and friends have contributed more than $1 billion to them since July, bringing deposits from all sources to more than $9 billion, CNBC said.

Claiming an account

At 2:52 a.m. EST Friday Musk quoted with one word, "True," a post by a parent explaining that Trump Accounts are not only for newborns: "Any child under 18 with a Social Security number can have one. The $1,000 seed money is only for kids born between 2025 and 2028." The post said parents still need to claim and activate accounts before they can receive outside contributions, "including the SpaceX shares that Elon Musk and the company pledged to active accounts."

Two launches in 11 hours

SpaceX's mission page lists USSF-380 for 11:39 p.m. EST Saturday from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg, with a four-hour window and a backup opportunity on Sunday. The first-stage booster is flying for the 27th time and is to land on Of Course I Still Love You, stationed in the Pacific Ocean. About 11 hours later, a second Falcon 9 is to send Northrop Grumman's Cygnus XL on the NG-25 resupply mission from Space Launch Complex 40 in Florida, with capture at the station planned for Tuesday, according to NASA.

Starship status

Booster 25 and Ship 46 kept taking shape at Starbase on Wednesday: Booster 25's FX:3 section was stacked onto F2:4 in Mega Bay 1, and Ship 46's common-dome section was stacked in Mega Bay 2, the Starship Desk log shows, with both moves marked confirmed. Booster 24 and Ship 44, assigned to Flight 17, rolled back to the Mega Bays on Tuesday after cryogenic proof testing at Massey's.

Ship 43, assigned to Flight 16, is receiving engines inside Mega Bay 2, and the Gigabay at Starbase reached a construction milestone with the removal of its final tower crane, a Thursday video update said, according to the Starship Desk log, which marks both reports unconfirmed. Flight 16 remains listed no earlier than Dec. 10 from Starbase Pad 2, a target SpaceX has not confirmed.

III · Starmind & Starlink

Inside the Starmind license

A 70-meter first satellite, six orbital shells and three objections turned down; astronomers say they are "disheartened and dismayed."

The first-generation Starmind satellite, designated AI1, is substantially larger than today's Starlink spacecraft and is designed to span roughly 70 meters in one direction and 20 meters in the other with its solar arrays and radiators deployed, NASASpaceFlight reported from the FCC order. The license covers six orbital shells between about 500 and 1,002 kilometers: three inclined around 26 to 32 degrees and three in sun-synchronous orbits of roughly 97 to 99 degrees, which can offer longer periods of sunlight for the solar arrays. The satellites will send data home mainly through laser links to the existing Starlink constellation.

Missing any deployment milestone voids the authorization, the order requires ongoing reports on conjunctions, deorbiting and spectrum use, and it deferred some disposal waivers for satellites above 600 kilometers pending further filings, NASASpaceFlight said. The FCC denied petitions filed by Amazon Leo, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society (AAS). About 28,800 satellites have been launched since Sputnik in 1957 and about 17,200 remain active, more than 11,100 of them Starlink spacecraft, according to Jonathan McDowell's catalog as of Nov. 18.

Astronomers object

The AAS said on Wednesday it was "disheartened and dismayed" by the approval, Al Jazeera reported. "The AAS is gravely concerned about the future of federally funded astronomical research under such a fundamentally altered night sky," the society said, adding: "we remain concerned by the lack of formal coordination requirements in the license granted to SpaceX in this case." According to the same report, SpaceX describes the plan as "the fastest way to scale given the growing difficulty of finding electrical power sources on Earth."

Half-price Starlink

Starlink offered 50% off Residential service for six months to customers who sign up by Cyber Monday, Nov. 30, posting at 2:15 p.m. EST Thursday that the deal is "available in select areas."

Iberia Express takes off

"Our first plane with Starlink has already taken off. Soon, all the others," the Spanish airline Iberia Express posted on Thursday (translated), promising "totally free" high-speed Wi-Fi included in the booking. Starlink replied at 11:16 a.m. EST that it now offers "fast and reliable connectivity" on board the airline (translated).
