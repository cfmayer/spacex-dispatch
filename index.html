The Morning Picture

USSF-481 flies, Dragon docks, and SpaceX lends Tesla its thrusters

A Falcon 9 launched the classified USSF-481 mission from Vandenberg at 8:01 p.m. EDT Thursday, 17 minutes into its window, and the booster landed on its 28th flight, Spaceflight Now reported. An hour later, at a SpaceX test site in McGregor, Texas, Tesla's new Roadster flew on SpaceX cold-gas thrusters. "Tesla x SpaceX," Elon Musk posted.

Cargo Dragon docked at the space station Thursday morning. Ukraine says its Starlink and Starshield service is back to full capacity. Musk says the Star Mind orbital computer SpaceX is building with Nvidia reaches volume production next year, and SpaceXAI testifies to the New York City Council at 10 a.m. EDT today. SPCX fell 2.33% to $148.62.

Today · 10 a.m. EDT

SpaceXAI before the New York City Council

Fri Oct 16 · 10:00 a.m. EDT · Committee on Technology, Council Chambers, City Hall

The hearing is an oversight session on "the risks posed by artificial intelligence" (Council calendar). The Council subpoenaed Musk and SpaceXAI on September 28; Anthropic, Google, Meta and OpenAI testified at an October 5 hearing that SpaceX skipped, and SpaceX agreed to appear after the Council said it would ask a judge to enforce the subpoena, CNBC reported. The Council has not said who will testify for the company, amNY reported.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

A classified payload, and a Dragon at Harmony

USSF-481 went south from California, and Dragon delivered the station's last new solar arrays.

USSF-481

The rocket, also listed as TH-2 in public notices, took a southerly path; hazard warnings suggest a low Earth orbit inclined about 80 degrees, Spaceflight Now reported. Booster B1081 landed on Of Course I Still Love You a little more than eight minutes after liftoff, that ship's 228th landing and the 671st Falcon booster landing. B1081 has also flown Crew-7, CRS-29, PACE and 16 Starlink missions (SpaceX). It was the second "TH" mission in as many months, after USSF-259 on September 16.

CRS-35 at the station

"Docking confirmed – Dragon has arrived at the @Space_Station!" SpaceX posted at 10:29 a.m. EDT Thursday. The cargo ship carries more than 6,300 pounds, including the last pair of ISS Roll-Out Solar Arrays, and is to stay about a month (NASA).

Rocket parts in a car

"SpaceX has used thrusters to position spacecraft for decades. Making them work in a vehicle instead of a vacuum took all-new hardware & controls," Tesla posted. The Roadster's thrusters were supplied by SpaceX and rated at more than 80 kilonewtons, and the event was held at SpaceX's McGregor testing facility, Car and Driver reported. Some watchers saw the collaboration as Musk building interest in merging the two companies, Reuters reported.

III · Starlink

Ukraine says service is back to full capacity

The restriction lasted more than a day; who ordered it is still unclear.

Ukraine's Starlink and Starshield services, slowed earlier this week, have been restored to full capacity, a Ukrainian official told Axios. The official also said Ukraine struck a Russian oil refinery overnight, defying President Trump's demand that it stop. "It's still unclear to many inside the Trump administration and the Ukrainian government whether Trump ordered the satellite restrictions," Axios reported. A defense ministry source also told the Kyiv Independent that service had been restored.

Quick Hit

In the air. "Starlink is really that good," Musk wrote, quoting a frequent Delta flyer who switched to United for its Starlink Wi-Fi, and he shared United's tool for checking whether a flight has it.

IV · AI & Compute

Star Mind, "far better than almost anyone expects"

Nvidia's Jensen Huang says the two companies are designing a server for space.

"One particular new market that we're going to go explore together is putting data centers out in space," Huang said on the BG2 podcast, adding that the companies' engineers are creating "a new type of server, a new type of architecture that might be super good in space and on Earth," Tesla News Wire reported. "Old friends are the best friends. Jensen rocks," Musk replied. "The Star Mind computer being co-engineered by SpaceX and Nvidia is far better than almost anyone expects and will reach volume production next year." Asked about a goal of 10 gigawatts of compute by the end of 2027, he wrote, "Maybe more than 10GW."

SpaceX has said the first-generation Starmind satellite is based on an optimized Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 system, with launches as early as the fourth quarter of 2027, Benzinga reported. Its IPO filing warned that orbital AI compute involves "unproven technologies, and may not achieve commercial viability," Reuters reported in April.

Quick Hit

Grok Bot. "Never seen anything grow as fast as @Grok @Bot! And there is no free tier," Musk posted. Of Anthropic giving evaluators from METR employee-like access to assess its safety practices, he wrote, "SpaceX will probably do the same."

V · Filings: SEC, FAA & FCC

Quiet at the SEC; Starbase comments run to November 30

No new SEC filings or FAA Federal Register notices since Thursday's issue; the Starbase review remains open.

FAA

  • Notice of intent · Oct 14 — The FAA will prepare a Programmatic Environmental Impact Statement (PEIS) on SpaceX's proposal to increase Starship-Super Heavy operations at the Boca Chica launch site at Starbase and to add Starship landing and expenditure locations (docket FAA-2026-12808; 91 FR 65289). Later license changes and temporary airspace closures fall within its scope. Federal Register
  • What is proposed — Phase 1 allows up to 100 launches and 200 landings a year, at any time on any day; Phase 2, expected within several years, raises the cap to 456 launches and 912 landings, with launches on weekdays only, Invezz reported. The current limit is 25 launches and 50 landings. Four in-person scoping meetings are set for November 17 and 19 and a virtual one for November 18; comments are due November 30, SpacePolicyOnline reported.
  • Notice of intent · Sep 29 — Scoping comments on Starship at Space Launch Complex 37, Cape Canaveral (up to 76 launches a year), closed October 14 (docket FAA-2026-10792). Federal Register
