The Morning Picture

SpaceX in talks for Boost Mobile

SpaceX is in talks to buy EchoStar's wireless service Boost Mobile or gain access to its network, which EchoStar leases from AT&T and T-Mobile, The Washington Post reported Monday, citing people familiar with the negotiations. The talks have run for months and are about launching SpaceX's own wireless service through a deal with EchoStar, Reuters wrote, summarizing the report.

The goal is a hybrid satellite-and-ground network under the Starlink Mobile name to compete with AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile (Reuters). Musk approached the heads of the top U.S. telecom companies, including Verizon, in recent months to ask for access to their networks, according to The Washington Post, which called a deal "a potential back door into the $235 billion wireless market." SpaceX and EchoStar did not immediately respond to requests for comment from Reuters.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Falcon 9 flies for the 700th time

Starlink 15-18 closed out October with SpaceX's 11th orbital launch of the month.

A Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base at 12:04 a.m. EDT Saturday, Oct. 31, with 27 Starlink satellites (Spaceflight Now); Friday's issue had listed an 11:48 p.m. Friday target. It was the 700th Falcon 9 launch to date and SpaceX's 11th and final orbital flight of October (Spaceflight Now).

Booster B1093 flew for the 18th time and landed on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You about 8.5 minutes after liftoff, the 675th Falcon booster landing to date (Spaceflight Now). SpaceX confirmed deployment of all 27 satellites at 1:08 a.m. EDT. "Congrats to the @SpaceX team on over 700 Falcon 9 flights!" Musk posted at 9:32 a.m. Saturday.

Falcon's pace slows as Starship nears

SpaceX "is starting to wind down the Falcon program as it turns its focus to Starship," Spaceflight Now wrote. The Starlink 15-16 launch on Oct. 27 was the first time since July 2025 that SpaceX flew fewer than 150 orbital launches in a 365-day period, down from a peak of 171 in December 2025, and the company stopped flying Starlink missions on Falcon 9 from Florida in August (Spaceflight Now).

Since then, the Florida launches have included NASA's SpaceX Crew-13, CRS-35 and Bandwagon-5, which flew in October. SpaceX is expected to start flying orbital Starship missions regularly in 2027 (Spaceflight Now).

Starship: both catches on Flight 15

Starship Flight 15, with Ship 42 and Booster 22, is expected in mid-November from Pad 2 at Starbase, Texas, and SpaceX plans to try to catch the ship as well as the booster (Spaceflight Now). An unofficial range slide puts it no earlier than Nov. 12 (Space Notices); SpaceX has not announced a date. "We expect Starship to fly weekly at some point in the first half next year and then second half next year, twice weekly or maybe even more frequently by the end of next year," Musk said on the third-quarter earnings call (Spaceflight Now).

Flight 16, with Ship 43 and Booster 23, is planned as the first Starship launch from Launch Complex 39A in Florida. Booster 23 could be ready for a static fire in late November and Ship 43 in mid-December, and the first flight from the pad could come in late January, NASASpaceflight estimated. SpaceX "rounded off an impressive week of testing involving the flight vehicles for Starship's next two missions," NASASpaceflight posted Saturday.

Artemis III: Blue Origin goes first

Artemis III will start with a New Glenn launch carrying Blue Origin's Blue Moon lander, which can wait in orbit for roughly 30 days, before NASA commits the Space Launch System and Orion, NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman told NASASpaceflight. "We're not going to launch without Blue Origin in it," he said.

The mission is planned as a checkout and rendezvous campaign in low Earth orbit. Starship is expected to provide high-definition imagery, controllability testing and rendezvous and docking practice over roughly two weeks, and Isaacman named the lander as the main remaining schedule bottleneck (NASASpaceflight).

Moon Base update Nov. 7. NASA leadership will share "the latest progress" on its Moon Base plans on Nov. 7, NASA's Moon Base account announced, in a post NASA shared Saturday.

III · Starlink

"Starlink saves lives"

A rescue team with no phone signal took its call through a Starlink dish in its control vehicle.

"Starlink saves lives," Musk posted at 8:12 a.m. EST Sunday, sharing an August rescue in England. Penrith Mountain Rescue Team was training at Mardale Head in Cumbria on Aug. 9 when the North West Ambulance Service called; "due to lack of signal around Mardale Head this was received via Starlink in our control vehicle," the team wrote.

A walker had tumbled down a steep bank into a hornets' nest near Wetheral. Twenty-seven team members worked with Cumbria Fire and Rescue Service and the ambulance service to haul the casualty up the bank on a stretcher by rope, in a callout that lasted 3 hours 45 minutes (Penrith Mountain Rescue Team).

Shotwell gives 2 million shares to children

"Robert and I are thrilled that our gift of over 2 million shares of SpaceX stock will be distributed to children's Trump accounts in December!" SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell posted at 4:30 p.m. EDT Friday. The first shares go to 11- to 17-year-olds in zip codes with a median family income of $57,000 or less, she wrote, as well as to children in zip codes with too few residents for a median income to be reported. "These families should receive a notice by Monday," she added.

"In a few weeks, an additional gift will be finalized for eligible 11 to 17-year-old children in military families serving overseas, children in foster care, and more children whose MFI is $90,000 or less living in counties near our home in the great state of Texas," Shotwell wrote, using the abbreviation for median family income. She thanked SpaceX, the Treasury, the Internal Revenue Service and the Dell Foundation, among others. "Tesla and SpaceX have created meaningful wealth for hundreds of thousands of employees over time and improved the retirement savings of hundreds of millions of people," Musk posted at 11:24 a.m. EDT Saturday. "Cool," Musk replied at 6:11 p.m. EDT.

IV · AI & Compute

Colossus 2: 1.2 million Blackwells by year end

Musk walked the site twice and laid out four buildings of Nvidia GB300 chips, two still to come online.

"Macroharder and Minihard both have 220k GB300s with 800 gig nics configured for training. Gun Range will have the same. Spam (used to house Hormel products) will have ~200k GB300s, but configured for inference, so much simpler networking and less than half the fiber," Musk posted at 1:45 p.m. EDT Saturday. "Good progress by the SpaceX team and contractors. Gun Range is tracking to be operational by end of November and Spam in December."

"There will be a total of around 1.2M Blackwells at Colossus 2 by end of year," he added. On Sunday at 3:32 p.m. EST he wrote: "The other supercompute projects are unable to reach their goals, but SpaceX is already achieving ours."

Why Grok Bot has usage limits

"The cost of serving Grok Bot was unsustainably high relative to revenue," Musk replied at 3:34 p.m. EST Sunday to a user asking why usage ran out quickly. At 6:20 p.m. he posted: "We land a few dozen fixes, features and sometimes powers to Bot 7 days a week and we look at your feedback to make Bot as perfect as possible." Earlier, at 3:23 p.m., he wrote that "Grok @Bot will research gifts that your friends and family would love and buy them," quoting a user who said the bot picked and bought a present with up to $100 from an X Money account.

Quick Hit

Orbital compute by 2028. "Orbital compute deployed per month probably exceeds terrestrial around the end of 2028 and then far exceeds it in 2029," Musk posted at 10:41 p.m. EDT Friday.

Google's TPU answers from orbit

Google's Project Suncatcher prototype satellite booted its Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) chip in orbit, ran a prompt and sent back "a haiku," Google CEO Sundar Pichai posted at 1:05 p.m. EDT Friday. "Cool," Musk replied.
