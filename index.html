The Morning Picture

Crew-12 splashes down, Starlink signs up whole airlines, and Elon Musk takes his India fight public

The Dragon capsule Freedom splashed down off Los Angeles at 11:34 a.m. EDT Thursday, bringing four Crew-12 astronauts home after 237 days at the International Space Station (ISS). Starlink had a strong day with airlines: American Airlines will put it on its entire mainline fleet of more than 1,000 jets, KLM will offer it free on long-haul flights from mid-2027, and Royal Air Maroc became the first African airline to sign on.

In India, Elon Musk accused "certain oligarchs" of blocking Starlink's license, and the government called the claim baseless. On Wall Street, a report that SpaceX is seeking about $40 billion of debt to buy Nvidia chips helped push SPCX down 2.51% to $167.60 on Wednesday, ending a three-day run. In Washington, the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) put two direct-to-device spectrum proposals on its October 29 agenda. SpaceX has filed nothing new with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) since Wednesday morning.

I · Human Spaceflight & Launches

Crew-12 is home after 237 days

Dragon Freedom undocked Wednesday morning and landed in the Pacific on Thursday; a Space Development Agency launch and the next station cargo run follow within days.

“
LIVE: After 237 days and 100 million miles in orbit, Crew-12 is returning to Earth. Watch with us as they splash down in the Pacific at 11:34am ET (1534 UTC).
NASA · @NASA · Thursday, October 8

Splashdown off Los Angeles

Freedom undocked from the station at 8:05 a.m. EDT Wednesday and splashed down at 11:34 a.m. EDT Thursday, NASA said. Aboard were NASA astronauts Jessica Meir and Jack Hathaway, European Space Agency (ESA) astronaut Sophie Adenot and Roscosmos cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev. SpaceX posted "Splashdown of Dragon confirmed!", and Elon Musk (@elonmusk) replied "Welcome home!"

Space Development Agency launch slips to Saturday

"Now targeting no earlier than October 10 for Falcon 9 to launch @SemperCitiusSDA's fourth Tranche 1 data transport mission. The rocket and payload remain healthy, and additional time allows for double checks on vehicle systems and range deconfliction"
SpaceX · @SpaceX · post, Wed 8:34 p.m. EDT

The mission carries 21 satellites for the Space Development Agency (SDA) Transport Layer from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. The new target is 3:29 a.m. EDT Saturday, October 10. Date change: the launch had been set for early Friday, October 9; the FAA's launch advisory issued Thursday still showed that 3:36–4:41 a.m. EDT window, with Saturday as the backup. SpaceX's stated reason is the double checks and range deconfliction quoted above. Before that, an automatic abort on October 5 was followed by delays from October 6 and October 8, Spaceflight Now reported.

Next station cargo: Tuesday

NASA set the 35th Commercial Resupply Services mission (CRS-35) for no earlier than (NET) 6:33 a.m. EDT Tuesday, October 13, carrying more than 6,300 pounds, including the final pair of ISS Roll-Out Solar Arrays (NASA).

Starship @NASASpaceflight

Ship 40, which flew Flight 13 in July and made the first intact ocean landing, was returned to Starbase Wednesday morning. The outlet also posted its latest unofficial schedule:

"Starship (unofficial) schedule latest: Flight 15 (Starbase Pad 2) - NET November 8. Flight 16 (Potentially KSC 39A) - NET December 10."
NASASpaceflight · @NASASpaceflight · post, Thu 10:59 a.m. EDT

III · Starlink

Three airlines in two days

American goes fleet-wide, KLM makes it free, Royal Air Maroc brings it to Africa, and the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) clears a much larger direct-to-cell network.

1,000+American Airlines mainline jets to get Starlink, installs from early 2027
1 GbpsPeak speed per Performance Aero Terminal, per American
15,000Next-generation direct-to-cell satellites approved by the FCC on October 6
$100New Starlink referral reward, to both the referrer and the friend

American Airlines: every mainline jet

American said Thursday it will install Starlink across its whole mainline fleet of more than 1,000 aircraft starting in early 2027. That expands a May commitment covering more than 500 Airbus jets. Each Performance Aero Terminal delivers up to 1 gigabit per second (Gbps).

"Starlink will deliver fast, reliable connectivity across @AmericanAir's full mainline fleet so passengers can stream, game, work, and stay connected to family and friends throughout their travel"
Starlink · @Starlink · post, Thu 9:46 a.m. EDT

KLM and Royal Air Maroc

KLM announced free high-speed Starlink Wi-Fi on intercontinental flights from mid-2027, rolling out in phases through mid-2028; CEO Marjan Rintel said it will keep passengers connected over oceans and remote routes. Musk posted "Starlink coming to KLM!" A day earlier, Royal Air Maroc said it will be the first African airline with Starlink, starting in 2027.

Stephanie Bednarek @SRBednarek · vice president of commercial sales, SpaceX

Starlink Mobile, the direct-to-cell service, launched in Bangladesh with the carrier Banglalink on Wednesday.

"We're now connecting millions of people in Bangladesh with Starlink Mobile! Our technology provides an additional layer of connectivity in cellular dead zones so coastal communities, remote businesses, families and travelers can stay in touch no matter where they are."
Stephanie Bednarek · @SRBednarek · post, Wed 8:26 p.m. EDT

FCC clears a bigger direct-to-cell network

The FCC approved up to 15,000 next-generation direct-to-cell satellites in very low orbit, plus a waiver letting SpaceX offer service without leasing spectrum from a ground-based carrier, Sawyer Merritt reported, citing PCMag. Musk added (conditions and deadlines are in Section VI):

"Starlink Mobile (direct-to-cell) V2 satellites are incredible. This @SpaceX constellation will enable more than 100 times the bandwidth of our current V1 system!"
Elon Musk · @elonmusk · post, Wed 11:21 a.m. EDT
Quick Hit

Starlink raised its referral reward on Thursday to $100 for both the referrer and the friend, paid as cash or credit after two months of service (details). Rewards vary by market.

IV · The India Standoff

"Is Ambani the real boss of India?"

Musk escalated his complaint over Starlink's stalled Indian license, India's government pushed back, and the opposition leader joined in.

On Wednesday morning, Musk quoted a DogeDesigner post arguing "India needs @Starlink." The post cited 11,256 villages without 4G coverage as of May 2026 and said only 4.26% of homes have wired internet. Musk replied:

"Unfortunately, we are being blocked by certain oligarchs in order to maintain their monopolistic chokehold on the Indian people. You can guess who they are … This is a crime against the people of India!"
Elon Musk · @elonmusk · post, Wed 11:23 a.m. EDT

Later Wednesday, answering a user who said Starlink terminals had been recovered from insurgents in Manipur, he wrote: "Starlink beams are turned off over India, so the claim that they were used by anyone for any reason is absolutely false."

The government's answer

Officials called the charge baseless and said the licensing process is non-discriminatory. They said Starlink, Eutelsat OneWeb and Jio Satellite are all at the same security-assessment stage, and that spectrum will be assigned after clearance (The Hindu; Economic Times). Business Standard listed three items still pending: security clearance, foreign direct investment approval and spectrum rules.

Thursday: Musk names a name

Quoting Aniruddha Malpani, who wrote that "The government is more interested in protecting the interests of crony capitalists than of citizens," Musk posted:

"Starlink is licensed in over 165 countries and has spent five years complying with every single law and requirement of the government of India, so why still no license? Is Ambani the real boss of India?"
Elon Musk · @elonmusk · post, Thu 9:25 a.m. EDT

Rahul Gandhi (@RahulGandhi), Leader of the Opposition in India's lower house, replied: "Welcome to India, Elon. Wait till you discover the other guy." Musk answered: "Thank you, Rahul. This is indeed troubling." He also posted: "People who oppose @Starlink hurt only the least-served. The wealthy or those living in large cities already have good Internet, so they don't understand."

V · AI & Compute

A reported $40 billion for Nvidia chips

SpaceX is said to be lining up debt backed by the graphics processors themselves; neither company has confirmed it.

The Financial Times first reported the talks. According to CNBC, SpaceX is in preliminary discussions with Apollo and several banks on about $40 billion of financing, mostly investment-grade debt, with the graphics processing units (GPUs) as collateral. TipRanks reported a split of roughly $10 billion in bank loans and $30 billion in investment-grade debt. SpaceX sold $25 billion of bonds two weeks after its June initial public offering (IPO) and carried about $40 billion of debt in its second-quarter financials, CNBC said. Its Colossus and Colossus 2 data centers hold about 230,000 and 550,000 chips, with a target of 1.21 million by late December. SpaceX also has computing deals with Google, Anthropic, Reflection AI and Cursor.

On the product side, Musk said most requests to the Grok assistant "are pretty simple and will be handled by a lightning-fast version of Grok 4.8 when that comes out," and, replying to Aaron Burnett (@aaronburnett), CEO of Mach33, wrote "The path to a petawatt/year is mass drivers on the Moon."

Quick Hit

Morgan Stanley reiterated Overweight, CNBC reported, quoting the note: "The next chapter of the SpaceX story is its role in converting energy (e) into intelligence (i) through physical robots (r) manufactured on/near shore." The target is $300 (Stocktwits).

Quick Hit

Elon Musk @elonmusk: "Thank you on behalf of the amazing people of SpaceX, Tesla, Neuralink and Boring Company, without whom anything I have done would have been impossible" (Thursday, 11:56 a.m. EDT).

VI · Filings: SEC, FAA & FCC

Nothing new at the SEC or FAA; the FCC sets a D2D agenda

The FCC's October 6 direct-to-device license came with deadlines, and the agency added two related spectrum votes for October 29.

No filings for Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (CIK 0001181412) were accepted on the SEC's Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval system (EDGAR) between 8:00 a.m. EDT Wednesday and 1:00 p.m. EDT Thursday. Latest insider filings:

FAA

No new FAA licenses, license modifications or environmental notices for SpaceX appeared on the FAA's commercial space license pages or in the Federal Register since Wednesday morning. Latest on record:
  • Notice of intent · Sep 29 — The FAA will prepare a Supplemental Environmental Impact Statement (SEIS) on Starship operations at Space Launch Complex 37: up to 76 launches and 152 landings a year. Public scoping comments close Wednesday, October 14 (docket FAA-2026-10792; 91 FR 61551). Federal Register · FAA

FCC

  • New · Oct 7 · Open-meeting agenda — FCC Chairman Brendan Carr set two votes for October 29: a notice of proposed rulemaking to make 482 MHz more spectrum eligible for Supplemental Coverage from Space (direct-to-device service), and a proposed auction of 25 MHz in the 1675–1695 and 2020–2025 MHz bands, usable for 5G, direct-to-device or both. SpaceX had asked for 2020–2025 MHz; the October 6 order deferred that request. Draft texts were not yet public. Telecompetitor · Broadband Breakfast
  • Order DA 26-1078 · Oct 6 · docket 25-340 — The Space and Wireless Telecommunications bureaus granted in part SpaceX's 15,000-satellite direct-to-device system. Half (7,500) must be operating by October 7, 2032, and all by October 7, 2035. SpaceX must post a surety bond by November 4 and has 30 days from release to decline the conditioned grant. The order waives the terrestrial-lease requirement and defers the 20.2–21.2 and 30–31 GHz bands. Exterra · Telecompetitor
