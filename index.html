The Morning Picture

FCC proposes 482 MHz more for satellite-to-phone service

The Federal Communications Commission on Thursday voted to propose making an additional 482 megahertz of spectrum available for Supplemental Coverage from Space, the satellite-to-phone service behind Starlink Mobile, expanding the eligible bands from five to 13 (FCC). The proposal would also eliminate the rule's leasing requirements and its geographically independent area requirement, replacing them with flexible license areas and coexistence agreements between satellite operators and carriers, and seeks comment on direct-to-device performance requirements. "With today's proposal, we take our most comprehensive step yet to modernize the FCC's rules for direct-to-device," Chairman Brendan Carr said, according to the FCC.

It is a notice of proposed rulemaking: the next step is public comment, and final rules would need another vote. AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile had wanted 290 MHz cut from the plan, Teslarati posted. "Streamlining rules and ensuring access to critical radio frequency (RF) resources are more important than ever," said Tom Stroup, president of the Satellite Industry Association (SIA).

III · Starlink

Starlink starts pricing by neighborhood

Residential plans in select areas will move up or down by as much as 20% with local supply and demand, reviewed about quarterly.

"Starlink prices [are] dynamically based on supply of capacity and demand from customers," SpaceX told Residential customers in an email reported by PCMag on Thursday. "Given this rapid pace of change, we will be reviewing prices on a regular basis, roughly quarterly, and you may see more frequent changes than we've made historically or compared to other providers. But these won't just be increases: as we expand capacity, service pricing in your area may be lowered."

Users in the Midwest, including Kansas and Missouri, reported the emails. In Rolla, Missouri, the Residential Max plan now defaults to $156 a month on Starlink.com, up from $130; in Kansas City, Missouri, it is $117, and in Marysville, Kansas, $104, PCMag found. Code on Starlink's website suggests differences are limited to 20% up or down, and changes take effect with the next billing cycle on or after November 30 (PCMag; Sawyer Merritt).

Quick Hit

$69 a month. Starlink's average revenue per user was $69 a month across 13.6 million consumer subscribers in the third quarter, down from $83 a year earlier, PCMag noted.

SpaceX accuses T-Mobile of trying to harm Starlink Mobile

"Unfortunately, the misplaced desire to harm a competitor has led many incumbents to oppose modernized rules that would bring direct-to-cell service to underserved border communities," SpaceX said in a new filing to the FCC, posted by Sawyer Merritt at 2:51 p.m. EDT Thursday. "SpaceX has worked extensively with other operators, including T-Mobile," the filing said, and it urged the commission to treat AFTRCC, the Aerospace and Flight Test Radio Coordinating Council, as "now purely a lobbying organization whose mission is to protect legacy companies."

IV · AI & Compute

Musk: Grok 4.8 "will not be a disappointment"

The next Grok model is late, he said, and is not being tuned for benchmarks; Terafab "is going well."

"I know it's taken longer than expected, but Grok 4.8 will not be a disappointment. We're not optimizing for benchmarks and I really don't care how Grok does on those, but it will be incredibly useful," Musk posted at 1:17 a.m. EDT Friday.

"Worth noting that Grok @Bot has no free tier," he wrote at 5:39 p.m. Thursday, quoting a post on its daily active users rising for 16 consecutive days, and at 12:33 a.m. Friday he posted: "Hire Grok @Bot to be an employee."

Suppliers praise Terafab's pace

"The Terafab project is going well," Musk posted at 1:12 a.m., quoting a summary of a The Elec opinion piece. The Korean outlet wrote that Tesla and SpaceX procurement staff visited about 10 Korean supplier booths at SEMICON West and that more than half signed non-disclosure agreements, with a target of 1 million wafers a month within 10 years.

"Even when we send inquiries in the early morning hours in the U.S., we receive responses within 30 minutes to an hour," one supplier official told The Elec. "I've never seen this level of execution speed in the semiconductor industry."

Quick Hit

"Everyday is Zeroday." Musk posted that at 6:33 p.m. EDT Thursday after the theft of about $6.4 billion from Binance hot wallets (Elliptic). At 11:12 p.m. he added: "Super intelligence agents will at some point do this to gain currency for themselves, not a human."
