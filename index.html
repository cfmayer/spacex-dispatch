The Morning Picture

FAA opens a full environmental review of 456 Starship launches a year from Starbase

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) published a notice today that it will prepare a programmatic environmental impact statement on SpaceX's plan to raise Starship operations at Starbase, Texas, to as many as 456 launches and 912 landings a year, from 25 launches today. Public scoping meetings are set for November 17 to 19, with comments due November 30. SPCX fell 4.21% to $156.54 on Tuesday, the day the plan became public.

Ship 40, the first Starship to come back to its launch site after flying, now stands in a Megabay, where SpaceX says it will yield "first-of-its-kind data" on a flown heatshield. Cargo Dragon is due at the space station Thursday, the same evening a Falcon 9 is scheduled to launch USSF-481. SpaceXAI agreed to testify before the New York City Council on Friday. There were no new SEC filings.

I · Starship & Launches

Ship 40 goes vertical for a teardown of a flown heatshield

No SpaceX launches since CRS-35 on Tuesday morning; the returned Starship is now indoors at Starbase.

Ship 40 in the Megabay

"The first Starship to return to its launch site, Ship 40's journey took it across air, space, and sea," SpaceX posted at 5:52 p.m. EDT Tuesday. "Now that it is vertical alongside three other Starships in a Megabay at Starbase, it will provide first-of-its-kind data on a flown Starship heatshield, Raptor engines, avionics, and more." Ship 40 made the program's first intact ocean splashdown on Flight 13 in July and kept floating; crews towed it to Christmas Island after 24 days adrift, took heatshield samples there and shipped it to Texas, TeslaNorth reported. Musk shared the return as well.

CRS-35 en route

Cargo Dragon, launched at 6:33 a.m. EDT Tuesday with the station's last pair of roll-out solar arrays, is set to dock autonomously on Thursday at about 11 a.m. EDT, SpaceX said. Its booster landed at Landing Zone 40. Falcon 9 has flown more than 110 times this year and is on pace for about 150 launches, against 165 in 2025, Invezz reported.

III · Starlink

Top marks in rural Britain

"Try @Starlink Internet!" Musk wrote on Tuesday, quoting a post saying Starlink ranked first for both download speed and consistent quality among rural users in the UK in a study by network analytics firm Opensignal, ahead of EE, Three and Vodafone. According to that post, Starlink also led rural download speeds in all 14 European markets studied. The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) approval of SpaceX's 15,000-satellite direct-to-device system still carries a surety-bond deadline of November 4 (Section V).

IV · AI & Compute

SpaceXAI will testify in New York on Friday

SpaceXAI agreed to appear before the New York City Council's Committee on Technology on Friday, October 16, at 10 a.m. EDT, ending a standoff that began with a September 28 subpoena, amNY reported. The company skipped the council's October 5 hearing, where OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta testified under oath; the council then said it would ask a state judge to enforce the subpoena. The council has not said who SpaceXAI will send. "Conspicuously absent was SpaceXAI, which is unacceptable," Speaker Julie Menin said in a statement on Tuesday.

On X, Musk spent much of Tuesday promoting Grok Bot features, including LEGO sets he said Grok Bot had bought. Replying to a post on a Morgan Stanley note about SpaceX as a distributed inference cloud, he wrote: "True, this is a very big deal."

V · Filings: SEC, FAA & FCC

FAA starts the Starbase review

A notice of intent in today's Federal Register opens scoping on up to 456 Starship launches a year; nothing new at the SEC.

FAA

  • Notice of intent · Oct 14 — The FAA will prepare a Programmatic Environmental Impact Statement (PEIS) on SpaceX's proposal to increase Starship-Super Heavy operations at the Boca Chica launch site at Starbase and to add Starship landing and expenditure locations (docket FAA-2026-12808; 91 FR 65289). Later license changes and temporary airspace closures fall within its scope. Federal Register
  • What is proposed — Phase 1 allows up to 100 launches and 200 landings a year, at any time on any day; Phase 2, expected within several years, raises the cap to 456 launches and 912 landings, with launches on weekdays only, Invezz reported. The current limit is 25 launches and 50 landings. Four in-person scoping meetings are set for November 17 and 19 and a virtual one for November 18; comments are due November 30, SpacePolicyOnline reported.
  • Notice of intent · Sep 29 — Scoping comments on Starship at Space Launch Complex 37, Cape Canaveral (up to 76 launches a year), closed today (docket FAA-2026-10792). Federal Register
